    Price discovery and the order book#

    • Price is the last trade, not fair value. It moves when aggressive orders exhaust the liquidity resting at the best price.
    • Liquidity is the ability to trade size without moving price; thick books mean tight spreads and small slippage.
    • Order flow imbalance drives short-term moves: more market buys than resting asks lifts price.
    Bids (buyers)        Asks (sellers)
100 @ 50.00          200 @ 50.01
300 @ 49.99          150 @ 50.02
500 @ 49.98          400 @ 50.03

Market buy 350 → 200 @ 50.01, 150 @ 50.02
Best ask is now 50.03: price moved 2 ticks.

    This is why large orders are sliced (TWAP, VWAP algorithms) and why a thin book lets one order spike price.

    Bid, ask, spread and slippage#

    • Bid = best price buyers pay; ask = best price sellers accept; spread = ask − bid, the cost of an instant round trip.
    • Slippage = fill worse than the quote, from thin size at the quote, fast markets or a large order.
    • Spreads widen with low volume, news, small caps, exotic pairs and off-hours.
    • Budget at least half the spread on entry and exit; scalping in wide-spread names can erase the whole edge.

    Microstructure in one card#

    • Price-time priority: best price fills first; at the same price, earlier orders fill first.
    • Maker vs taker: limit orders that rest add liquidity (often cheaper); market orders take it.
    • Gaps form when news arrives while the market is closed; stops can fill far away.
    • Venues: exchanges (central order book), ECNs, OTC dealers, dark pools for block trades.
    • Your edge as a retail trader is in setup selection and risk control, not speed.

    Dow theory: six tenets#

    1. The averages discount everything: all known information is in the price.
    2. Three movements: primary trend (1+ years), secondary reactions (weeks to months, retracing about a third to two thirds), minor moves (days).
    3. Primary trends have three phases: accumulation, public participation, excess (and distribution, public selling, panic in bear markets).
    4. Averages must confirm each other (originally Industrials and Transports).
    5. Volume confirms the trend: it expands in the direction of the primary trend.
    6. A trend persists until a clear reversal (a failed swing that breaks the sequence of highs and lows).

    03Price action: levels, trendlines and structure

    TradingOverview/technical-analysis/handbook/02-price-action

    Support and resistance#

    • Support: where buying has appeared before (prior lows, round numbers, volume nodes). Resistance: where selling has appeared.
    • Why levels work: memory, unfilled orders left behind, and attention on obvious numbers.
    • Strength: sharp reversals, high volume, several timeframes, and recent tests. Many tests can also exhaust a level.
    • Role reversal: broken resistance tends to become support, and broken support resistance.
    • Treat levels as zones, not exact prices.

    Takeaway Levels work through memory, unfilled orders and attention; strength comes from sharp reversals, touches and volume.

    Trendlines and channels#

    • Uptrend line joins higher lows; downtrend line joins lower highs. Two touches draw it, a third validates it.
    • Do not force lines through every wick. Steeper lines break sooner.
    • Channels: a parallel line through the opposite swings. Buy the lower rail, sell the upper, or trade a break with confirmation.
    • A break of a trendline signals the trend is weakening, not necessarily reversing.
    • Dynamic levels: 20/50/200 EMAs and VWAP act as moving support in uptrends.

    Liquidity grabs and stop hunts#

    • Stops cluster just beyond obvious highs, lows and round numbers.
    • Price often sweeps those levels, triggers the stops, then reverses, leaving a long wick.
    • Setup: sweep below support, strong close back inside, then long with the stop below the wick.
    • Defence: place stops beyond the obvious level with a buffer (or use a close beyond the level as the exit).
    • Most "manipulation" is ordinary liquidity mechanics; plan for it instead of blaming it.

    Candle anatomy and psychology#

    • Body = open to close; green or white when close > open. Body size shows conviction.
    • Upper wick = high − max(open, close): sellers rejected higher prices.
    • Lower wick = min(open, close) − low: buyers rejected lower prices.
    • Close near the high = buyers in control; close near the low = sellers in control.
    • Small body (doji-like) = indecision. It matters only after a strong move or at a level.
    close location = (close − low) ÷ (high − low)1 = closed on the high · 0 = closed on the low

    Takeaway Body shows conviction, upper wicks rejected highs, lower wicks rejected lows, and the close location tells who won.

    Context beats the pattern#

    • Level: a hammer at support or a higher low means far more than one mid-range.
    • Trend: a reversal candle against the higher-timeframe trend is a lower-odds trade.
    • Volume: above-average volume on the signal candle adds conviction.
    • Sequence: a doji after three large down candles at support is exhaustion; after one candle it is noise.
    • Confirmation: wait for the next candle to close in the signal's direction before entering.

    Nison's extra patterns#

    • Belt-hold: opens on its extreme and runs the other way (bullish opens on the low); stronger the longer the body.
    • Upside-gap two crows: after a rally, a small red gaps up and a second red engulfs it but stays above the first candle; bearish.
    • Tasuki gaps: a gap in the trend direction, then a counter candle that does not close the gap; continuation.
    • Rising / falling three methods: a long candle, three small counter candles inside its range, then a long candle to a new close; continuation.
    • Doji family: long-legged (big wicks both sides), gravestone (open = close = low; bearish at tops), dragonfly (open = close = high; bullish at bottoms), four-price (no range; illiquid).
    • Nison's rule: candles give early warnings, Western tools give targets. Pair a candle with a trendline, MA or level.

    Indicators in Python (pandas)#

    import pandas as pd

def ema(s, n):
    return s.ewm(span=n, adjust=False).mean()

def rsi(close, n=14):
    d = close.diff()
    gain = d.clip(lower=0).ewm(alpha=1/n, adjust=False).mean()    # Wilder smoothing
    loss = (-d.clip(upper=0)).ewm(alpha=1/n, adjust=False).mean()
    return 100 - 100 / (1 + gain / loss)

def macd(close, fast=12, slow=26, signal=9):
    line = ema(close, fast) - ema(close, slow)
    sig = ema(line, signal)
    return line, sig, line - sig                                   # line, signal, histogram

def atr(high, low, close, n=14):
    prev = close.shift()
    tr = pd.concat([high - low, (high - prev).abs(), (low - prev).abs()], axis=1).max(axis=1)
    return tr.ewm(alpha=1/n, adjust=False).mean()

def bollinger(close, n=20, k=2):
    mid = close.rolling(n).mean()
    sd = close.rolling(n).std(ddof=0)                              # population std, as charting tools use
    return mid - k * sd, mid, mid + k * sd

def vwap(high, low, close, volume):                                # one session of intraday bars
    tp = (high + low + close) / 3
    return (tp * volume).cumsum() / volume.cumsum()

    Checked against the ta library: MACD, Bollinger and VWAP match exactly; RSI and ATR match after the warm-up bars. Wilder smoothing is an EMA with α = 1/N.

    Elliott wave basics#

    • Impulse: 5 waves with the trend (1-2-3-4-5), then a correction of 3 waves (A-B-C).
    • Hard rules: wave 2 never retraces beyond the start of wave 1; wave 3 is never the shortest; wave 4 does not overlap wave 1's price territory.
    • Guidelines: wave 3 is often 1.618 × wave 1; waves 2 and 4 alternate (sharp vs sideways); wave 5 often equals wave 1.
    • Corrections: zigzag (5-3-5, sharp), flat (3-3-5, sideways), triangle (3-3-3-3-3).
    • Typical retracements: wave 2 retraces 50–62%, wave 4 about 38%.
    • Useful as a map of where you are in a trend; counts are subjective, so trade the invalidation levels.

    Wyckoff in practice#

    • Three laws: supply and demand (price), cause and effect (the length of the range sets the size of the move), effort vs result (volume vs price progress).
    • Phases A–E: A stops the prior trend, B builds the cause, C tests (spring or upthrust), D shows strength or weakness, E is the trend out of the range.
    • Spring trade: long after the false break below the range closes back inside; stop under the spring low; target the range top, then the measured cause.
    • Upthrust trade: the mirror image at the top of a distribution range.
    • Effort vs result: heavy volume with little price progress means the other side is absorbing.

    Backtest steps#

    1. Rules: unambiguous entry, stop, exit and size. Every vague word becomes a bug.
    2. Data: enough history (5–10 years daily), adjusted for splits and dividends, survivorship-free if picking stocks.
    3. Simulate: signal on bar t, fill on bar t+1 open; include brokerage, taxes (STT in India), spread and slippage.
    4. Record: every trade, then compute metrics.
    5. Validate: out-of-sample, walk-forward, other symbols, parameter neighbourhoods.
    6. Forward test: paper or minimum size before scaling.

    A vectorised backtest without look-ahead (pandas)#

    import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def backtest_sma(close, fast=20, slow=50, cost_bps=10):
    """Long when fast SMA > slow SMA. Signal on today's close, trade on the next bar."""
    signal = (close.rolling(fast).mean() > close.rolling(slow).mean()).astype(int)
    position = signal.shift(1).fillna(0)                 # no look-ahead
    ret = close.pct_change().fillna(0)
    trades = position.diff().abs().fillna(0)             # 1 on every entry or exit
    strat = position * ret - trades * cost_bps / 10_000  # cost per side
    equity = (1 + strat).cumprod()
    years = len(close) / 252
    cagr = equity.iloc[-1] ** (1 / years) - 1
    drawdown = equity / equity.cummax() - 1
    sharpe = np.sqrt(252) * strat.mean() / strat.std()
    return {"CAGR": cagr, "MaxDD": drawdown.min(), "Sharpe": sharpe,
            "Trades": int(trades.sum()), "Exposure": position.mean()}

def position_size(account, risk_pct, entry, stop):
    risk_amount = account * risk_pct
    per_share = abs(entry - stop)
    return int(risk_amount // per_share)                 # round down

position_size(1_000_000, 0.01, 500, 480)                 # 500 shares

    The single shift(1) is what separates an honest backtest from a fantasy: the signal uses today's close, the position starts tomorrow. Costs are charged on every entry and exit.

    Production safety#

    • Kill switch: flatten all positions and halt at a daily loss limit.

    • Reconnect logic for data feeds and token refreshes.
    • Run on a server (VPS or cloud), not a laptop.
    • Version control; separate dev, paper and live; never hot-fix a live bot after a loss.
    • Keep API keys out of code and repositories.
