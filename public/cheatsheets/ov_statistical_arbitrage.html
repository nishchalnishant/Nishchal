    01Foundations and honesty

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Statistical Arbitrage · chapters 1–4 (what stat arb is, the database, data biases, research vs real markets)

    What statistical arbitrage really is#

    spread = log A − (α + β · log B) β from OLSAR(1): Δs_t = a + b · s_(t−1)half-life = −ln 2 / ln(1 + b) (only when b < 0)
    • Classic arbitrage: same cash flow, two prices, no risk. Stat arb has no guarantee, only a statistical tether. "Trade a stationary relationship, not a price."
    • BAJFINANCE vs BAJAJFINSV, 2,473 days: return correlation 0.824, β 1.153, yet spread ADF p = 0.199 (random walk not rejected) and half-life 104 days. Rolling correlation ranged 0.71–0.92.
    • Dollar-neutral (+1 / −1) leaves net beta +0.09 here; beta-neutral sizes the short at βA/βB ≈ 1.06× for net beta ≈ 0. Neutrality drifts and re-hedging costs money.

    The family tree, and where the edge went#

    FormTrades
    Single-name reversionA stock vs its own average (fragile)
    PairsTwo stocks, one hedge ratio
    BasketsA cointegrated weighted group
    Cross-sectional, factor-neutralA whole universe ranked, market and sector hedged
    • The edge came from providing liquidity, slow news diffusion and structural flows (rebalances, redemptions, tax selling); early quant desks harvested it in the late 1980s–1990s.
    • It decayed with faster data, crowded cointegration screens, ETFs and market makers. August 2007's "quant quake" was crowded books unwinding into each other.
    • In India, shorting beyond a day isn't free: stock futures, SLB borrow or intraday square-off, each with its own costs, margin and availability, on both legs, both ways.

    Building the historical database#

    • Download once, store locally, read the cache every run, verify before trusting. Universe: the 50 Nifty stocks (NSE) plus NIFTY (NSE_INDEX) as the trading calendar, tagged into 14 sectors.
    • Ten years of daily bars: source="db" 54.7 ms vs source="api" 346 ms (≈ 6.3×), and the cache returns identical bytes each run while vendor history can be revised.
    • DuckDB tables: market_data (symbol, exchange, interval, epoch-second timestamp) and data_catalog (first/last date, rows, last download).
    • Verify: rows per symbol (≈ 2,477 for full history), start dates for young listings or renames, stale series (> 7 days behind), and internal gaps against the NIFTY calendar (10 of 50 names were perfectly clean; worst missed 3 sessions). One missing bar on a leg fakes a spread move.
    • Nightly top-up: fetch after each symbol's last date, re-fetch the last few weeks for revisions, upsert on symbol + date, back off on timeouts and HTTP 429s.

    • Liquidity (365-day close × volume): even the thinnest names trade ₹160–200 crore a day, so the filter ranks cost rather than feasibility. A pair is only as liquid as its thinner leg.

    • Name your biases: survivorship (delisted and dropped names missing), symbol changes (demergers stitched into one series), and no point-in-time index membership. Blind dropna() silently favours old large caps.

    Research vs real markets: the short leg decides#

    Short vehicleHow longCosts and catches

    SLB borrowDays to weeksAnnual borrow fee, thin patchy supply, recall mid-trade

    model fair future: F = S · e^((r − q)T) (r 6.5%, q 0.6%: RELIANCE 30 days ≈ ₹6.41 ≈ 48.6 bps)
    • Backtests silently assume you can stay short as long as reversion takes, both legs fill at the measured close, and holding is free. In India all three are false.

    • Choose the short vehicle first: it sets the cost. Learn on closes; never assume you can trade at them.

    02Stationarity, correlation and cointegration

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Statistical Arbitrage · chapters 5–7 (stationarity, correlation is not cointegration, cointegration mechanics)

    Random walks vs stationary series#

    random walk I(1): x_t = x_(t−1) + e_t variance grows as t·σ²AR(1) I(0): y_t = φ·y_(t−1) + e_t, |φ| < 1 variance → σ² / (1 − φ²)log return: r_t = log P_t − log P_(t−1) turns I(1) into I(0)
    • Across 400 simulated paths, random-walk variance reached 391.9 by step 399 and kept growing; AR(1) with φ = 0.85 settled at 3.56 (theory 3.60). Bounded variance is the anchor you trade.
    • RELIANCE, 2,477 days: log-price mean jumps 6.27 → 7.16 between halves (non-stationary); returns stay stable. Volatility clustering is not non-stationarity, but it matters for sizing.
    • Prices are I(1), returns I(0); stat arb manufactures an I(0) spread and trades that.

    ADF, KPSS and Hurst#

    RELIANCEADF (H0: unit root)KPSS (H0: stationary)
    Log price−2.234 vs 10% CV −2.567: can't reject7.216 vs 1% CV 0.739: reject
    Returns−15.241 vs 1% CV −3.433: reject0.317 vs 5% CV 0.463: can't reject
    • The two tests have opposite nulls: agreement is a strong verdict; disagreement means re-specify (trend, breaks). Fix ADF's regression form (c, ct, n) and lag rule before running.
    • Power trap: a truly stationary AR(1) with φ = 0.99 is detected only ≈ 61% of the time. "Failed to reject" often means too little data.
    • Hurst: ≈ 0.5 random walk, < 0.5 mean-reverting, > 0.5 trending. 14 NSE names ranged 0.46–0.56: no stock is a convincing mean-reverter. One clue only.
    • Across 24 names, 100% of price series kept a unit root and 100% of return series rejected it.

    Correlation is not cointegration#

    Pair (NSE 2016–2026)Return correlationEngle–Granger pVerdict
    TATASTEEL / JSWSTEEL0.76 (highest)0.204Not cointegrated
    KOTAKBANK / HDFCBANK0.600.001Cointegrated
    MARUTI / M&M0.530.000Cointegrated
    • Correlation (on returns) is same-day co-movement, useful for hedge stability. Cointegration (on log levels) asks whether the hedged spread keeps returning, which is what a pairs trade needs.
    • 75 same-sector pairs → 11 cointegrated; 0 of 4 cross-sector "fake" pairs. Rank correlation between return correlation and cointegration p: −0.04, essentially none.
    • Spurious regression: 3,000 pairs of independent random walks gave |t| > 1.96 in 90.1% of runs (should be 5%), median |t| 10.0. High R² on price levels means nothing.
    • Verdicts are window-specific: INFY/HCLTECH p 0.007 in the first half, 0.861 in the second. Screen on cointegration first, use correlation as a secondary filter, rank on half-life and cost, confirm out of sample, and correct for the number of pairs tested.

    Engle–Granger mechanics on HDFCBANK vs KOTAKBANK#

    Hedge-ratio convention (log prices, 2019–2023)β
    OLS A on B (used for the spread)1.0545
    OLS B on A, flipped1.2724 (20.7% higher)
    Total least squares (symmetric)1.1751
    product of the two OLS slopes = R² (1.0545 × 0.7859 ≈ 0.83) → never reciprocalsOU half-life: regress Δs on s(t−1): λ = −0.0237 → κ = 0.0237 → ln 2 / κ = 29.3 days
    • Step 1: OLS of log A on log B gives β and the residual spread. Step 2: test the spread. The pair passed with coint p = 0.0306 (t −3.521 vs 5% critical −3.341); the runner-up HDFCBANK–SBIN failed at 0.096.
    • Report the honest p: a plain ADF on the residual says 0.0075, but β was estimated, so Engle–Granger's 0.0306 is the number. The hedge ratio is an estimate with a convention, not a fact (errors in both legs bias OLS).
    • Spread sd 0.0698 log units, 59 mean crossings, near-symmetric tails. ≈ 2.4% of a gap closes per day.
    • Half-life mirage: test stationarity first. A random walk can print a 280-day "half-life"; a near-unit-root drift 64 days with ADF p 0.106. A rolling 120-day β swung −0.31 to 1.30 (range 258% of its mean): a fixed in-sample β can drift out of bands.

    • 6,000 simulated random-walk pairs gave a 5.25% false-positive rate: the test is honest; the danger is how many times you run it.
    • "Choosing what not to test is the cheapest risk control in the field." Fix window, universe, sector map, history minimum, correction and split before looking, and report the whole funnel. Zero survivors is a legitimate result.

    The brutal reality check#

    View (frozen β 1.0545, 60-day z, CNC costs 0.474% a round trip)SharpeTotal
    In-sample gross1.14+134.2%
    In-sample net1.01+110.5%

    Out-of-sample net (2024 →, 2.5 years)0.34+11.5% (max drawdown −13.8%)

    • Leak 1, out of sample: about two-thirds of the edge vanished.
    • Leak 2, costs: 8 fills a round trip, dominated by the 0.1% sell-side STT; 33 trips × 0.474% = 15.9% of capital. Out-of-sample net Sharpe hits zero near a 1.9% round-trip cost.
    • Leak 3, de-cointegration: rolling 252-day p ranged 0.005–0.978 (latest 0.734) and failed the 5% test in ≈ 90% of windows; the frozen β was ≈ 63% above the test-period average, leaving directional risk.
    • Leak 4, look-ahead and survivorship: full-sample z normalisation inflated OOS Sharpe 0.34 → 1.12; same-bar fills flip +135% into −64%; this pair was the best of 10 bank pairs, and 5 of the 10 lost money net out of sample.

    04Doing it properly: dynamic hedges, baskets, books and risk

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Statistical Arbitrage · chapters 11–14 (Kalman hedge ratios, baskets and Johansen, cross-sectional factor-neutral, risk, sizing and portfolio)

    Kalman filter hedge ratios#

    state x = [α, β] drifts: x_t = x_(t−1) + w, Q = δ / (1 − δ) · Iobserve: A_t = α_t + β_t · B_t + v, variance Rinnovation e_t = A_t − (α + β·B_t) (the dynamic spread)only the Q / R ratio matters · defaults δ = 1e-4, R = 1e-3
    HDFCBANK on KOTAKBANK, netIS SharpeOOS SharpeTrades
    Static β+1.12+0.7641
    Kalman β−0.53−1.5078

    Kalman β was smooth (0.64–1.07, never negative) while rolling OLS swung −0.31 to +1.74 and went negative on 7.5% of days, yet the static β won the trade test; 0 of 20 Q × R settings beat it out of sample. "Adaptivity is not free": every re-hedge pays costs on both legs, and the free intercept can absorb the very mispricing you want. Freeze δ and R on in-sample data before any out-of-sample look.

    Baskets and the Johansen test#

    • A basket has 3–5 legs and k − 1 free weights: pooled reversion, but more estimation error and cost (≈ 69 bps a round trip for five legs vs ≈ 28 for a pair intraday).
    • Johansen fits a VECM on log levels and finds the cointegration rank r: eigenvectors are the basket weights. Trace tests rank ≤ r stepwise; max-eigenvalue tests r vs r + 1. Fix det_order and lags in advance.
    • Five banks, 2019–2023: trace said r = 1 (75.18 > 69.82, then 45.56 < 47.85); max-eigen said r = 0. Pre-declare which rule wins.
    • Weights with HDFCBANK at +1: ICICI −0.166, KOTAK −0.518, AXIS −0.115, SBIN −0.017; spread ADF p 0.0005, half-life 23.7 days (optimistic: fitted on the same data).
    • Split at mid-2021 and three of four weights flipped sign (SBIN −0.80 → +8.56); bootstrap noise exceeded the weights themselves. Out of sample the same vector's p went 0.0005 → 0.558; 16 sub-baskets were 94% stationary in-sample and 0% after. Use the fewest legs, never trade the in-sample winner.

    Cross-sectional, factor-neutral books#

    residual = return − β · NIFTY return (120-day rolling β)signal = −(sum of last k residuals), demeaned across stocksweights: rank, demean, scale to gross 1 (≈ ½ long, ½ short)
    • Removing market cuts a median 31% of daily variance; market plus sector 61%. The book trades the remaining third. Sector averages are only known at the close, so act next bar.
    • Top-minus-bottom decile +3.89 bps a day gross; gross Sharpe 0.51 (market-only) and 1.34 (market + sector); realised book beta ≈ 0.006.
    • But turnover ≈ 235× a year: gross edge ≈ 2.48 bps a day vs cost ≈ 6.45 → net Sharpe −1.99. Breakeven is 2.66 bps a side vs realistic 6.9 (MIS) or 11.9 (CNC). Slowing the signal (k = 15) cut losses to −0.16 but never crossed zero.
    • Real capacity needs hundreds of names and a dependable short side; crowded unwinds can flip reversal into momentum (2007).

    Risk, sizing and portfolio construction#

    Pair (2019–23)EG p
    M&M / MARUTI0.0015
    INFY / TCS0.0017

    HDFCBANK / KOTAKBANK0.0306

    • Six pairs (half-life 5–60 days) vs one: OOS Sharpe 0.43 vs 0.34, max drawdown −10.0% vs −13.8%. Shared legs (TCS, TATASTEEL) mean fewer independent bets than it looks.
    • Vol targeting (leverage = target vol ÷ own vol, 8% per pair, 10% book, frozen on train) lifted Sharpe 1.10 → 1.34; cap any pair at 35%; risk parity gives each 1/6 of risk. Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage buys stability, not correctness; correlations near 0.04 can jump toward 1 in a crisis.
    • Rupee-neutral is not beta-neutral: pair beta = β_A − hedge × β_B (M&M/MARUTI −1.11). A NIFTY futures overlay can trim residual beta.

    • Retire a pair after 3 rolling readings with p > 0.10; re-admit after 3 with p < 0.05. Retirement always lags the break: median live pairs were 0 of 6.

    The validation battery#

    LayerResult (HDFCBANK / KOTAKBANK)
    Single in-sample backtestNet Sharpe 1.01
    Walk-forward, anchored (14 folds of 126 days)Median 0.47; 93% of folds positive; worst −0.96
    Walk-forward, rollingMedian 0.57; 86% positive; best +3.07

    Deflated Sharpe (64 trials)PSR vs 0 = 1.000; DSR = 0.962
    Probability of backtest overfitting (CSCV, 8 blocks)54%: selection adds little
    Block bootstrap (20-day blocks, 3,000 resamples)OOS 0.34, 95% CI [−0.47, 1.15]; ≈ 20% chance the true Sharpe is below 0
    Stability sweepBase 0.82; 3 × 3 neighbourhood 0.79 (sd 0.08); 96% of cells positive
    E[max SR of N trials] ≈ √Var(SR) · [(1 − γ)·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N·e))], γ ≈ 0.577PSR = Φ[(SR − SR*)·√(n − 1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR + ((γ₄ − 1)/4)·SR²)]
    • Shuffled k-fold leaks because trade labels overlap in time: 100% of test days leak; contiguous blocks 16.6%; purging ±holding period plus an embargo, 0%.
    • A ridge (thin spike) dies when a setting is nudged; a plateau is robust but can still be low: 90-day z → OOS 0.99, 60 or 120 → 0.34, tighter stop → 0.19, doubled costs → 0.22.
    • Run the battery once on a pre-fixed hypothesis; validators can be overfit too, and the true number of trials is always larger than the ones you wrote down. Here the surviving edge is thin and statistically indistinguishable from zero.

    Implementation pathways#

    RouteCost patternWatch-outs
    Cash intradayA full round trip dailySquare off near 15:20
    SLB borrowOne round trip + borrow (≈ 3% a year used)Lumpy, recallable, can spike to double digits
    Stock futuresOne round trip + roll every ≈ 30 daysBasis wobble, lot rounding, dividends
    • Holding period picks the vehicle: multi-day holds need futures or SLB; daily re-crossing overtakes futures cost after ≈ 1 day.
    • Intraday cost stack: effective spread ≈ 5.3 bps (Corwin–Schultz on liquid large caps; daily high-low overstates it ≈ 33×), legged entries median 7.8 bps (p95 16.2), square-root impact 8–26 bps at 1–10% participation → a round-trip hurdle ≈ 123 bps against a per-trade edge of 30–50 bps.
    • Legging: with 95% per-leg fill odds, ≈ 9.5% of entries leave a naked leg (≈ 18% at 90%); that tail outweighs slippage.
    • Kill-switch gates, any one flattens: |z| beyond the hard limit (4.8 in the sample), drawdown stop, broken leg (flatten the survivor now), thin margin or F&O ban, stale data, square-off clock. Retire decayed pairs (return correlation below 0.5 in 48% of history here) rather than averaging down. Rehearse in OpenAlgo's analyzer first.

    06Checklists and quick reference

    A "no" at any gate sends the idea back to research.

    The nine-gate pre-trade checklist#

    1. Liquidity: can both legs absorb your size without moving price?
    2. Borrow / short access: SLB or a liquid future, at a known cost?
    3. F&O eligibility and bans: on the list today and not in an MWPL ban, checked before every entry.
    4. Net costs: every charge, spread and impact on two legs, both ways; the edge must clear them.
    5. Fills and legging: next-bar fills, partials, broken-leg risk.
    6. Capital and margin: margin on both legs, daily MTM, a gap-day buffer.
    7. Monitoring: spread, z-score, exposure, P&L and broken-leg alerts.
    8. Kill switch: the flatten rule defined before you need it.
    9. Tax and entity: futures and cash are taxed differently; model after tax.
