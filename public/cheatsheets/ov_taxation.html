    01Start here: filing, buckets, regimes and deadlines

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Taxation · chapters 1–4 (why tax matters, the income buckets, old vs new regime, advance tax and deadlines)

    Why filing matters more than the tax#

    • Every exchange trade pays STT against your PAN, and the AIS (Annual Information Statement) compiles trades, dividends and interest from brokers, banks and exchanges. Your trades are already visible; filing just matches the records.
    • The Karnataka case: a farmer lost ≈ ₹26 lakh in F&O around 2014 and filed nothing because "no profit, nothing to report". In 2021 the case was reopened on ≈ ₹69 crore of turnover, a notice went unanswered, the whole turnover was treated as income, and the demand came to ≈ ₹68 crore.
    • A loss reported on time is harmless and becomes an asset: lose ₹2 lakh this year and next year's ₹3 lakh profit is taxed on ₹1 lakh.
    • File every year, on time, and answer every notice.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Why does an F&O loss still need a return filed on time?

      Show answer

      An on-time return lets the loss carry forward against future profits; an unfiled loss is lost, and unexplained turnover in the AIS can draw a notice.

    2. In the Karnataka case, what was taxed after notices went unanswered?

      Show answer

      The whole turnover (about ₹69 crore) was treated as income, giving a demand of about ₹68 crore, though the farmer had actually lost money.

    Takeaway Your trades are already visible through STT and the AIS; filing a loss on time is cheap insurance, and silence can turn turnover into a tax demand.

    The three buckets#

    ActivityBucketLosses can offset
    F&O on equity, commodity or currencyNon-speculative business incomeMost income heads, except salary
    Intraday equity (no delivery)Speculative business incomeOnly speculative income
    Delivery shares, equity mutual fundsCapital gains (short or long term)Only capital gains
    • Sorting test: delivery taken (even overnight) → capital gains; same-day, no delivery → speculative; any future or option → business, whatever the holding period.
    • "Speculative" is a tax label, not a judgement of skill. Business income is taxed at your slab rate.
    • Buckets don't net: an F&O profit of ₹1 lakh and an intraday loss of ₹40,000 leaves ₹1 lakh taxable, not ₹60,000. The wrong ITR form can make a return defective.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. F&O profit ₹1 lakh, intraday loss ₹40,000. What's taxable?

      Show answer

      ₹1 lakh. Speculative (intraday) losses can only offset speculative income, so the buckets don't net to ₹60,000.

    2. Shares bought today and sold tomorrow after delivery: which bucket?

      Show answer

      Capital gains (short term). Taking delivery, even overnight, moves it out of the speculative bucket.

    Takeaway Delivery means capital gains, same-day without delivery means speculative, any derivative means business; buckets don't net against each other.

    New regime slabs (FY 2025-26, default)#

    Taxable incomeRate
    Up to ₹4 lakhNil
    ₹4–8 lakh5%
    ₹8–12 lakh10%
    ₹12–16 lakh15%
    ₹16–20 lakh20%
    ₹20–24 lakh25%
    Above ₹24 lakh30%
    ₹16 lakh: 0 + 20,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 = ₹1,20,000+ 4% cess = ₹1,24,800
    • Each rate applies only to its slice. The 87A rebate (up to ₹60,000) makes slab tax zero up to ₹12 lakh (marginal relief just above), but it does not cover special-rate capital gains (20% STCG, 12.5% LTCG). Old regime rebate: up to ₹12,500 at ₹5 lakh or less.
    • Cess 4% on tax plus surcharge; surcharge starts above ₹50 lakh of income.
    • With business income, choosing the old regime needs Form 10-IEA by the Section 139(1) due date; miss it and you stay in the new regime that year.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Does the 87A rebate cover 20% STCG for someone earning ₹10 lakh?

      Show answer

      No. The rebate applies to slab tax only, not to special-rate gains such as 20% STCG or 12.5% LTCG.

    2. A trader with business income wants the old regime. What must they file, and by when?

      Show answer

      Form 10-IEA by the return due date; miss it and they stay in the new regime that year.

    Takeaway Each slab rate applies only to its slice; the 87A rebate zeroes slab tax up to ₹12 lakh but never covers special-rate capital gains.

    Advance tax and the dates you can't miss#

    DateCumulative share of the year's taxMeera (₹1.2 lakh tax)
    15 June15%₹18,000
    15 September45%+₹36,000
    15 December75%+₹36,000
    15 March100%+₹30,000
    31 July / 31 August (next year)ITR due date: 31 July for ITR-1/2; 31 August for non-audit business returns (ITR-3/4) from AY 2026-27
    • Advance tax applies when the year's net tax after TDS exceeds ₹10,000. Traders usually cross it because nothing is withheld on trading profits, and nobody sends reminders.
    • Each date tops you up to the running total (the September instalment is 30%, not 45%). Before each date, take broker P&L to date, estimate the tax, pay, adjust next time.
    • Two clocks: advance-tax dates avoid interest; the return due date protects loss carry-forward (up to 8 assessment years for business and capital losses). A late return still reports the loss but forfeits the carry-forward.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. What cumulative share of the year's tax must be paid by 15 September?

      Show answer

      45%. The September instalment itself is 30%, topping you up from 15% to 45%.

    2. When is a non-audit F&O trader's ITR-3 due from AY 2026-27?

      Show answer

      31 August. ITR-1 and ITR-2 stay at 31 July.

    Takeaway Above ₹10,000 of net tax, pay 15/45/75/100% by 15 June, September, December and March, and file by the due date to keep loss carry-forward.

    02The equity investor: capital gains, STT, intraday and gifts

    Capital gains on shares and mutual funds#

    Gain (listed shares, equity MFs)HoldingSectionRate
    Short-term (STCG)12 months or less111A20%
    Long-term (LTCG)More than 12 months112A12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year
    Debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 Apr 2023AnySlab rate (older units keep the earlier rules)
    grandfathered cost (bought before 31 Jan 2018) = max(actual cost, min(FMV on 31 Jan 2018, sale price))cost 15,000 · FMV 18,000 · sale 20,000 → cost 18,000 → LTCG 2,000 (not 5,000)
    • The ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption is per person per year across all equity, not per stock. Ravi's ₹2 lakh LTCG → ₹75,000 taxable → ₹9,375. Priya's 8-month ₹40,000 gain → ₹8,000. Add 4% cess (and surcharge at very high incomes).
    • Capital losses only offset capital gains: a long-term loss only LTCG, a short-term loss either. Carry forward 8 years if filed on time.
    • Each SIP instalment has its own purchase date. Use the broker's capital-gains statement for costs. Spread sales across years to reuse the exemption; hold winners past 12 months where practical.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Ravi has ₹2 lakh of LTCG on listed shares. How much is taxable?

      Show answer

      ₹75,000: the first ₹1.25 lakh a year is exempt, so tax is 12.5% of ₹75,000 (about ₹9,375) plus cess.

    2. Can a long-term capital loss offset a short-term gain?

      Show answer

      No. A long-term loss offsets only long-term gains; a short-term loss can offset either.

    Takeaway Listed equity: 20% if held 12 months or less, 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year if longer; the exemption is per person, not per stock.

    STT: a charge and a tracker#

    Transaction (from 1 Apr 2026, per the course)RatePaid by / on
    Delivery equity, buy and sell0.1%Both sides, trade value
    Intraday equity0.025%Seller, sell value
    Mutual fund units0.001%Seller
    Futures0.05%Seller, trade price
    Options sold0.15%Seller, on premium
    Options exercised0.15%Buyer, on intrinsic value

    Sell one ₹5 lakh futures lot → ₹250; sell one option lot at ₹50 × 65 → ≈ ₹4.88 (premium, not notional). 20 futures sales a month ≈ ₹60,000 a year. No separate STT return; it's collected through the broker. Traders reporting business income deduct STT and other charges as expenses (Vikram: ₹6 lakh profit − ₹45,000 STT = ₹5.55 lakh before other charges).

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. On which value is STT charged when you sell an option?

      Show answer

      On the premium received (0.15% per the course from April 2026), not on the contract's notional value.

    2. Can an F&O trader deduct STT?

      Show answer

      Yes. Traders reporting business income deduct STT with other trading charges as expenses.

    Takeaway STT is both a cost and a paper trail; on options it's charged on premium, not notional, and business traders deduct it as an expense.

    Intraday: speculative business income#

    • Same-day buy and sell with no delivery is speculative business income, taxed at your slab rate (₹1.2 lakh at a 10% slab → ₹12,000 + cess). Shares that settle into your demat aren't intraday, even if sold next day.
    • Deduct brokerage, STT and other trading charges. Most active traders file ITR-3.
    • Speculative losses only offset speculative income, carry forward just 4 years (vs 8 for business and capital losses), and need an on-time return.
    • Sunita: ₹50,000 intraday loss can't touch her ₹80,000 delivery STCG or her salary; next year's ₹30,000 intraday profit is absorbed, leaving ₹20,000 to carry.
    • Keep intraday and delivery records separate from day one.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Sunita's ₹50,000 intraday loss: can it reduce her delivery STCG?

      Show answer

      No. Speculative losses only offset speculative income; they can't touch capital gains or salary.

    2. How long can a speculative loss be carried forward?

      Show answer

      4 years, against 8 for business and capital losses, and only with an on-time return.

    Takeaway Intraday is speculative business income at slab rate; its losses offset only speculative income and carry forward just 4 years.

    Gifting and inheriting shares#

    TransferGift taxFuture income taxed to
    To an adult child or siblingNil (relative)The receiver
    To a spouse or minor childNilThe giver (clubbing, Sec 64)
    From non-relatives, > ₹50,000 in a yearWhole amount taxable (Sec 56(2)(x))The receiver
    Inheritance or willExemptThe heir
    • ₹50,000 is a cliff, not an allowance: ₹50,001 is taxed from the first rupee, and small non-relative gifts are added together.
    • The receiver keeps the donor's cost and holding period: shares bought for ₹1 lakh, held 3 years, gifted, sold 18 months later at ₹1.8 lakh → ₹80,000 long-term gain. Keep the donor's contract notes, or the cost may be unprovable.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. A friend gifts you ₹50,001 of shares. How much is taxable?

      Show answer

      The whole ₹50,001. ₹50,000 is a cliff, not an allowance, and small non-relative gifts in a year are added together.

    2. Shares gifted to your spouse earn dividends. Who is taxed?

      Show answer

      You, the giver, under the clubbing rule (Section 64).

    Takeaway Gifts to relatives are tax-free, but the receiver inherits the donor's cost and holding period; non-relative gifts over ₹50,000 a year are taxed in full.

    03The F&O trader: business income, turnover, audit and losses

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Taxation · chapters 9–13 (F&O is business income, turnover and expenses, tax audit, losses, not reporting)

    The number-one mistake: F&O is business income#

    Meera (correct)Rohan (wrong)
    Salary + F&O profit₹12 lakh + ₹3 lakh₹12 lakh + ₹3 lakh
    HeadBusiness incomeShort-term capital gains
    Expenses₹20,000 deducted → ₹2.8 lakhNone allowed
    Tax on F&O≈ ₹42,000 at the 15% slab≈ ₹60,000 at 20%, plus defective-return risk

    F&O profit is non-speculative business income ("profits and gains of business or profession") whether you trade monthly or hourly: slab rates, genuine expenses deductible, losses set off widely and carried 8 years if filed on time. Report it on ITR-3, never ITR-1 or as capital gains on ITR-2.

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Why does reporting F&O as STCG usually cost more?

      Show answer

      No expenses are allowed, it's taxed at 20% instead of the slab, and the return risks being defective.

    2. Which ITR form must an F&O trader use?

      Show answer

      ITR-3, never ITR-1, and not as capital gains on ITR-2.

    Takeaway F&O is non-speculative business income on ITR-3: slab rates, expenses deductible, losses set off widely; never report it as capital gains.

    Turnover is not contract value#

    F&O turnover = Σ |profit or loss on each trade| (ICAI 2022: option premium no longer added)trades +12,000 / −8,000 / +5,000 → turnover 25,000 · net profit 9,000 (taxed)
    • Anita's ₹3.2 crore of contract value was really ≈ ₹6 lakh of turnover. Turnover only decides audit thresholds; net profit is what's taxed. Take it from the broker's tax P&L and turnover report, not by hand.
    • Deductible if genuine and trade-connected: brokerage, STT, exchange charges, internet and phone (trading share), charting and data tools, CA fees, depreciation on a laptop (yearly share).
    • ₹1.5 lakh profit − ₹45,000 expenses = ₹1.05 lakh taxable, ≈ ₹6,750 saved at 15%. Keep contract notes, invoices and statements in one folder; never claim personal costs.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Trades of +12,000, −8,000 and +5,000. What are turnover and taxable profit?

      Show answer

      Turnover 25,000 (sum of absolute values); net profit 9,000 is what's taxed.

    2. Is option premium received added to turnover?

      Show answer

      No, not since the ICAI's 2022 guidance note; turnover is the absolute profit or loss on each trade.

    Takeaway F&O turnover is the sum of absolute profits and losses per trade; it only decides audit thresholds, while net profit is what's taxed.

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. ₹2 lakh F&O loss on ₹40 lakh of digital turnover: audit needed?

      Show answer

      No. It's far below the ₹10 crore threshold, and a loss alone doesn't force an audit.

    2. When can 44AD history bring in an audit at low turnover?

    Takeaway Turnover, not profit, triggers a 44AB audit (₹10 crore if nearly all digital); a loss alone never forces an audit.

    F&O losses: set-off and carry-forward#

    RaviAmount
    Salary₹8 lakh (never offset)
    Rent₹2 lakh → offset to 0
    FD interest₹1 lakh → offset to 0
    F&O loss₹5 lakh: ₹3 lakh used, ₹2 lakh carried
    Next year: F&O profit ₹3 lakhTaxable ₹1 lakh
    • In the same year an F&O loss offsets house property, other business, capital gains and other sources, but never salary.
    • Unused loss carries forward up to 8 assessment years, against future business income only, and only if the return was filed by the Section 139(1) due date. A late return keeps the current-year set-off but loses the carry-forward for good.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Can an F&O loss reduce tax on salary?

      Show answer

      No. It offsets house property, other business, capital gains and other sources, never salary.

    2. What does a late return cost an F&O loss-maker?

      Show answer

      The carry-forward. The current-year set-off survives, but the unused loss can't be carried to future years.

    Takeaway An F&O loss offsets everything except salary this year, then carries 8 years against business income, but only with an on-time return.

Report F&O every year on ITR-3 and reconcile with AIS first.

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. What is the penalty range for under-reporting income?

      Show answer

      50% to 200% of the tax on the under-reported amount (Section 270A in the old Act).

    2. Anjali lost ₹3 lakh on ₹40 lakh turnover. What does reporting cost her?

      Show answer

      Nil tax, and she keeps the loss to carry forward.

    Takeaway Not reporting F&O risks penalties of 50–200% of tax, notices from AIS mismatches, and losing loss carry-forward; reporting a loss costs nothing.

    04Global and crypto

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Taxation · chapters 14–17 (US stocks, Schedule FA and Black Money Act, crypto tax, crypto derivatives)

    US stocks for Indian residents#

    ItemRule
    Gains held > 24 monthsLong-term, 12.5% + cess
    Gains held ≤ 24 monthsShort-term, slab rate
    DividendsUS withholds 25%; India taxes at slab; claim foreign tax credit
    Remittances (LRS)20% TCS above ₹10 lakh a year (from 1 Apr 2025), credited back
    • Under the India–US treaty, residents' capital gains are taxed only in India. ₹8 lakh bought, ₹11 lakh sold after 30 months → ₹3 lakh gain → ₹37,500 + cess.
    • ₹10,000 dividend: ₹2,500 withheld in the US; Indian tax at 20% = ₹2,000, fully covered by the credit. File Form 67 before the ITR or the credit can be lost.
    • Don't apply the 12-month Indian rule to US shares, and treat TCS as a credit, not a cost.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. US shares sold after 18 months: how is the gain taxed?

      Show answer

      Short term, at your slab rate; long term needs more than 24 months for foreign shares.

    2. What must you file to claim credit for US dividend withholding?

      Show answer

      Form 67, before the ITR, or the foreign tax credit can be lost.

    Takeaway US shares are long term only after 24 months; dividends get a foreign tax credit via Form 67, and LRS TCS above ₹10 lakh is a credit, not a cost.

    Schedule FA and the Black Money Act#

    • Residents disclose every foreign asset every year in Schedule FA (US shares, foreign funds and ETFs, overseas brokerage and bank accounts), even with no sale, gain or dividend: it records what you own, not what you earned. ITR-2 for most; ITR-3 if you also trade as a business.
    • Black Money Act 2015 for undisclosed foreign income or assets: ₹10 lakh penalty per year of non-disclosure, 30% tax, and prosecution of 6 months to 7 years.
    • From 1 Oct 2024 the ₹10 lakh penalty doesn't apply if foreign assets (excluding immovable property) stay under ₹20 lakh, but the duty to disclose remains.
    • Meera held ≈ ₹90,000 of US shares, earned almost nothing and left Schedule FA blank: "no foreign income" isn't "no foreign asset". Fix past returns with a CA.
    • LRS example: ₹12 lakh remitted → ₹2 lakh above the ₹10 lakh threshold → ₹40,000 TCS, recoverable at filing.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. You hold ₹90,000 of US shares with no sale or dividend. Do you report them?

      Show answer

      Yes, in Schedule FA. It records what you own, not what you earned.

    2. ₹12 lakh remitted under LRS this year: how much TCS?

      Show answer

      ₹40,000: 20% on the ₹2 lakh above the ₹10 lakh threshold, recoverable at filing.

    Takeaway Disclose every foreign asset in Schedule FA every year, even with no income; the Black Money Act penalty is severe.

    Crypto: flat 30% and 1% TDS#

    RuleDetail
    Rate (Sec 115BBH)Flat 30% + surcharge + 4% cess; no slabs, no exemption
    DeductionsCost of acquisition only: no fees, software, power, interest
    LossesNo set-off against anything, even other crypto; no carry-forward
    TDS (Sec 194S)1% of sale value above ₹50,000 a year (specified persons) or ₹10,000 (others)
    ReportingSchedule VDA: ITR-2 as an investor, ITR-3 as a business

    Coin A gains ₹1 lakh → ₹31,200 tax; coin B loses ₹40,000 and can't reduce it: ₹60,000 real profit, ₹31,200 tax. TDS works like advance tax and leaves a trail in AIS / Form 26AS.

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Coin A gains ₹1 lakh, coin B loses ₹40,000. What's taxed?

      Show answer

      The full ₹1 lakh (about ₹31,200 with cess); the crypto loss can't offset it.

    2. Which expenses can be deducted from crypto gains?

      Show answer

      Only the cost of acquisition: no fees, software, power or interest.

    Takeaway Crypto gains are taxed at a flat 30% with only cost deductible; losses can't offset anything, even other crypto.

    Crypto derivatives: a grey area#

    Spot cryptoCrypto futures (common reading)
    RuleFlat 30%, Sec 115BBHUsually business income at slab
    ExpensesCost onlyBusiness expenses may be allowed
    LossesNo set-offSome set-off may apply
    1% TDSYesNo
    • Futures are contracts on a price, not transfers of the coin, so the 30% rule generally isn't read to reach them; a conservative view calls them speculative. No clear official guidance: ₹3 lakh profit could be ≈ ₹90,000 or ≈ ₹60,000 (at 20%) of tax.
    • LRS doesn't permit remitting margin to overseas exchanges or funding overseas derivatives, so offshore futures can breach FEMA regardless of tax paid.
    • Keep every record, document why you chose your position, and get a CA's review.
    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Why might crypto futures escape the 30% VDA rule?

      Show answer

      They're contracts on a price, not transfers of the coin, so the common reading treats them as business income.

    2. Can you send margin abroad under LRS to trade crypto futures?

      Show answer

      No. LRS doesn't permit funding overseas derivatives, so it can breach FEMA whatever tax you pay.

    Takeaway Crypto futures are usually read as business income rather than 30% VDA, but there's no clear guidance and offshore margin can breach FEMA.

    05Filing it right

    Which ITR form is yours#

    FormFitsCan't hold
    ITR-1 (Sahaj)Salary, interest, one house property, modest incomeCapital gains, any business income
    ITR-2Capital gains, multiple properties, foreign assets (FA), crypto as capital gains (VDA)Business income
    ITR-3F&O, intraday, trading or crypto as a business, plus everything ITR-2 holdsNothing: the complete form
    ITR-4 (Sugam)Limited presumptive casesMost active traders

    The kind of income, not the amount, picks the form, and business income overrides everything: Vikram's salary + ₹80,000 LTCG + ₹40,000 F&O profit → ITR-3; Anjali, an investor with no F&O → ITR-2. A mismatched form draws a Sec 139(9) defective-return notice, and ignoring it can make the return count as not filed.

    Check yourself 2 questions

    1. Salary, ₹80,000 LTCG and ₹40,000 F&O profit: which form?

      Show answer

      ITR-3, because business income overrides everything else.

    2. An investor with capital gains and foreign shares but no trading: which form?

      Show answer

      ITR-2.

    Takeaway The kind of income picks the form, and any business income, F&O or intraday, means ITR-3.

    Pre-filing checklist#

    1. Full-year broker P&L and turnover downloaded.
    2. Advance tax paid on the four dates where needed, visible in 26AS.
    3. Expense proofs saved and totalled.
    4. Figures agree with AIS and 26AS.
    5. F&O and intraday reported as business income on ITR-3.
    6. Schedule FA, Form 67 and Schedule VDA done where relevant.
    7. Regime chosen (Form 10-IEA if moving to the old regime with business income).
    8. Filed by the due date, so losses carry forward.
    9. CA review if turnover, audit, foreign assets or crypto apply.
    Check yourself 2 questions

      Show answer

      Form 67 for the foreign tax credit, Schedule VDA for crypto.

    2. Why does filing by the due date appear on the checklist?

      Show answer

      It's what preserves loss carry-forward.

    Takeaway Before filing: full-year P&L, advance tax in 26AS, expenses totalled, figures matching AIS, the right schedules, and an on-time return.
