    What a quant does: measure, then test for luck#

    • A quant measures before trusting a feeling, then asks whether the measurement is skill or luck. "A discretionary trader feels the trend; a quant counts it."
    • Research loop: hunch → falsifiable hypothesis → data → test → edge? Most ideas die here, and that is the filter working.
    • Toolkit: market structure, mathematics, code, and scepticism (assume the backtest is wrong until proven otherwise).
    NIFTY, 247 trading daysValue
    Up / down days128 (51.8%) / 119
    Average up / down day+0.58% / −0.65%
    Best / worst day+3.78% / −3.26%
    Excess kurtosis≈ 2.55 (normal = 0): fat tails

    Direction is close to a coin flip; any edge hides in the shape of the moves. Never assume returns are normal.

    Clearing, netting and settlement#

    • Novation: the clearing corp becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, so anonymous traders never face each other's credit.
    • Multilateral netting: 25 RELIANCE trades worth ₹33.0 lakh gross net to one obligation: deliver 300 shares, receive ₹3.94 lakh, 11.9% of gross.
    • T+1 counts exchange business days (Fri 26 Jun 2026 → Mon 29 Jun); fully in force since January 2023. Optional T+0 runs for a pilot list that keeps changing: check the current circular.
    • A failed short delivery goes to an exchange auction at the defaulter's cost; settlement must complete before a record date to get a dividend or bonus.
    1. Defaulter's own margin and collateral
    2. Settlement Guarantee Fund
    3. Clearing corp's own capital
    4. Non-defaulting members' funds, last

    That default waterfall is why margin is locked capital, not a fee.

    Simple vs log returns#

    simple r = P_t / P_(t−1) − 1 for people ("up 2%")log r = ln(P_t / P_(t−1)) for maths across timeΣ log r = ln(P_end / P_start) exactlyΠ(1 + simple r) − 1 the right way to chain simple returns

    NIFTY, one year: summed log returns −0.04169 (exact); summed simple returns −0.03324 (wrong); compounded simple −0.04084 (right). They agree for small moves and drift apart for big ones. Know which one you are holding.

    Four stylised facts#

    So: you can't reliably predict direction, but you can predict size. Durable strategies target risk. The leverage effect adds that volatility rises more after a fall than after an equal rise, so risk models must be asymmetric.

    Expectation, variance and the footprint of luck#

    E = win rate × avg win + (1 − win rate) × avg lossNIFTY, 730 days: 0.508 × 0.61% + 0.492 × (−0.62%) ≈ +0.008% a day
    • Expectation is the edge per bet and must be positive after costs; variance is the quality of the ride. Report both: same expectancy with a smoother path is the better strategy.
    • The law of large numbers only bites over many trades. A handful of winners proves nothing.
    • Monte Carlo: resample real NIFTY daily returns into 500 alternate years (seed fixed): growth of 100 ends at median 111, 5th percentile 87, 95th percentile 138. Same edge, very different lives. Use it for path questions with no clean formula: drawdowns, stops, risk of ruin.

    • Size to the worst day in your data and assume a worse one is coming; use half-Kelly or less; scale down when realised volatility rises; hedge the downside. Sample kurtosis is unstable: the next crash revises it upward.
    • Selling naked options for small frequent premiums is exactly the trade fat tails punish.

    Hypothesis tests and what p-values mean#

    • Every Sharpe, mean or win rate is an estimate: truth plus luck. Seven heads in ten flips doesn't prove a 70% coin.
    • Null (H0): no edge. p-value: how surprising the result would be if H0 were true. It is not the chance the strategy works, and p < 0.05 is not "95% sure".
    • 1,000 random long/short NIFTY strategies (no edge by construction): mean Sharpe ≈ 0, best +1.36, worst −1.32, and 10 cleared Sharpe 1.0 by chance. The more you try, the more extreme the best one looks.
    • Defences: test once on unseen data; count every try and raise the bar with it; demand an economic reason (risk premium, structural quirk, behavioural bias); prefer fewer, deeper hypotheses.

    "The goal of inference isn't to find a winner; it's to avoid being fooled by one."

    Stationarity, random walks and the EMH#

    NIFTY since 2021ADF statpVerdict
    Price−1.490.538Non-stationary (random walk)
    Daily returns−18.300.000Stationary
    • A random walk is today plus an unpredictable shock: no anchor. Model returns, not price levels; a price-level model breaks when price wanders somewhere new.
    • ADF's null is "unit root". p < 0.05 rejects it; a high p-value does not prove stationarity (statsmodels.tsa.stattools.adfuller).
    • EMH: weak (past prices useless), semi-strong (public news priced in), strong (private too). Cracks: momentum, value and low-vol anomalies, predictable risk, behavioural biases, structural flows (rebalancing, expiry).

    Efficient enough that easy edges vanish, not so efficient that none remain.

    Covariance and PCA: the hidden factors#

    portfolio return = R @ w portfolio variance = w′ C wcorrelation = cov / (σ₁ σ₂)
    • Eigenvectors of the covariance matrix are uncorrelated risk axes; eigenvalues are the variance along each.
    • Eight Nifty stocks: PC1 explains 41.6% with all-positive loadings (the market), PC2 22.7% is long IT, short banks: a sector found without labels. Mean pairwise correlation 0.31; INFY–TCS 0.78.
    • A long single-stock book is mostly a leveraged Nifty bet; hedging the market factor with index futures is the cheapest risk cut.
    • Structure drifts (PC1 can pass 60% in crashes) and covariance is noisy: use rolling windows and shrinkage, and treat factor labels as interpretations.

    Optimisation: convex is trustworthy#

    • Most quant models are "find the inputs that make one number best". Convex problems have one bowl, so any minimum is the global one; portfolio variance w′Σw is convex.
    • Constraints define the feasible region: weights sum to 1 (equality), long-only and caps (inequalities). A binding constraint costs objective value. Mean-variance is a convex quadratic program.
    • Five stocks, minimum variance: weights 14 / 25 / 17 / 35 / 9%, volatility 13.33% vs 13.87% equal-weight; the closed form Σ⁻¹1 / (1′Σ⁻¹1) matches when no constraint binds.
    • Expected returns are the most fragile input; minimum variance ignores them and is sturdier.
    • Ill-conditioned covariance (near-duplicate assets, too few days) gives huge unstable weights: shrink (Ledoit–Wolf), clean eigenvalues, add a ridge, never invert a rank-deficient matrix. If weights lurch on tiny data changes, fix the inputs, not the solver.

    The spread is the price of liquidity#

    • A chart shows past last-traded prices, not a price you can trade at. Microstructure is how orders become trades, and what that costs.
    • Measure costs in basis points (1 bp = 0.01%) so they compare across price levels. A spread can never be tighter than one tick.
    • MCX crude: spread ≈ ₹6 on ≈ ₹6,570 ≈ 9.1 bps, so a market-in, market-out round trip costs ≈ ₹91 per ₹1 lakh; ten a day ≈ ₹900–1,000 per lakh before any move.
    • RELIANCE spread proxy through one day: open ≈ 12.2 bps, midday ≈ 5.6, close ≈ 6.4. The U-shape means the open is often the most expensive minute.
    • A strategy netting 2 bps a trade is dead against a 9 bps spread. Backtests on mid or close prices promise edges live markets don't pay.

    The limit order book#

    • Bids rest below, asks above; nothing trades in the gap until someone crosses it. The next price comes from the book, not the last trade.
    • Crude example: best bid 6820 (2 lots), best ask 6822 (11 lots): a market buy fills at 6822, a market sell at 6820, and the 2-point gap is paid instantly.
    • Matching is price-time priority: best price first, then first-come at the same price. A large order eats several levels.
    • Imbalance = total buy qty ÷ (buy + sell qty): one weak, fast-flipping clue, distorted by hidden (iceberg) size.
    • OpenAlgo depth() returns five levels plus total buy and sell quantity; MCX keeps a live book into the night.

    Queue position is a perishable asset#

    queue value per lot = (spread / 2) × lot sizecrude: 1 / 2 × 100 = ₹50 a lot · ₹250 on 5 lotstime to front = depth ahead ÷ rate of sells hitting the bid = 60 lots ÷ 15 a minute = 4.0 min
    • Probability of a full 5-lot fill within 5 minutes ≈ 89% (Poisson arrivals). Counting cancels ahead (≈ 1.2 per sell) the wait drops to ≈ 2.4 minutes: visible depth overstates the queue.
    • Changing price or raising quantity sends you to the back; cutting quantity usually keeps your place.
    • India is FIFO, so early small orders beat big late ones (pro-rata venues reward oversized quotes instead).
    • Passive fills earn half a spread but suffer adverse selection: you tend to be filled just before price moves against you.

    Auctions and tick size#

    • Continuous trading is a double auction that always leaves a gap between bids and asks. A call auction collects orders without matching, then picks the single price that maximises matched quantity, and everyone gets that uniform price.
    • The official close drives index values, fund NAVs and benchmark-tracking trades.
    • Tick size sets the minimum spread and a cost floor: RELIANCE 0.1, MRF 5.0 (a share over ₹1 lakh), MCX crude 1.0. Too small a tick fragments the book; too large forces a wide spread. Ticks are not equal in percentage terms.

    Impact, slippage and TCA#

    impact ≈ Y · σ · √(order / ADV) Y ≈ 0.5RELIANCE (σ 131 bps, ADV 18.2 mn): ₹10 lakh ≈ 0.4 bps · 5% of ADV ≈ 15 bpsdoubling size → impact × 1.4, not × 2
    • Temporary impact is the cost of hurry and fades: trade slower. Permanent impact is information leakage and stays: trade quieter.
    • Measure slippage against the arrival (decision) mid, not VWAP. Implementation shortfall compares the real portfolio with a paper one filled instantly at arrival: spread, impact, drift and the cost of unfilled quantity.
    • Book walk, 40 crude lots: average 6572.70 vs mid 6570 = 4.1 bps (1.5 half-spread + 2.6 walking). Five visible levels understate big-order impact: a walk is a floor.
    • NSE's impact cost (≤ 0.50% for a set basket value) is a Nifty 50 eligibility test. Report TCA net of all charges, and never drop the bad days.

    The algo loop#

    1. Data → signal → sizing → order → risk, then repeat each bar or poll.
    2. Keep stages separate and testable: the signal never knows account size; sizing never knows the broker.
    3. "The signal proposes; risk disposes."
    • Polling (on a timer) is simple and fits minute-to-day edges; event-driven (each tick) is fast but brings reconnects, bursts and out-of-order messages. Pick the slowest clock your edge tolerates.
    • A bot is a state machine (flat, long, short): act only when the desired state changes, make orders idempotent so a retry can't double up, and treat the broker's book as the source of truth.
    • Sizing example: qty = min(₹1,00,000 ÷ price, 60): at ₹1,321.8 the budget allows 75 but risk caps it at 60. A 9/21 EMA cross on 1-minute RELIANCE made 21 decisions in one day: costs would eat it.

    Rebuilding the book from events#

    • "An order book is not a thing the exchange sends you": it is state computed from an ordered tape, deterministically.

    • Store arrival sequence numbers. After every event check: no crossed or locked book, no negative sizes, trades reconcile with the venue's tape.
    • Features (spread, depth per level, imbalance, microprice, trade sign) must use only events up to time t.
    • Replay answers queue and cost questions well, but cannot show how your own large order would have changed the future.

    Kill switches and fail-safe design#

    • Every order passes a serial pre-trade gate independent of the signal: kill switch → daily loss → position cap → rate limit → exchange. Any gate can veto; rejections are logged and alerted.
    • Limits in rupees off the live price, not share counts. Example: cap ₹20 lakh, loss limit ₹25,000, 4 orders per 10 s: 8 orders proposed, 4 passed (a BUY 600 would have made 1,600 shares ≈ ₹21.1 lakh).
    • A kill switch latches until a deliberate manual reset and logs who tripped it; auto re-arming is worse than none. Fail closed (missing quote = reject), add a dead-man heartbeat, and test the failure paths.
    • Runaway strategy: −₹5.67 lakh unmanaged vs −₹56,468 with a ₹50,000 switch: it caps losses but can overshoot. In 2012 one firm lost ≈ $440 million in ≈ 45 minutes. Exchanges require members to keep kill-switch capability.

    AR, MA and ARIMA: pick the right target#

    • AR(p): today depends on past values (φ); MA(q): on past shocks (θ); I(d): difference d times to reach stationarity. On returns (d = 0): y_t = φ·y_(t−1) + θ·ε_(t−1) + ε_t.
    • NIFTY daily returns, ARIMA(1,0,1): AR +0.002, MA +0.002, and the 5-day forecast collapses to the 0.044% mean after one step. Lag-1 autocorrelation 0.004, well inside the ±2/√n band. Near-zero coefficients are the right answer, not a bug.
    • Returns are close to white noise; piling on terms (ARIMA(5,0,5)) only fits noise. Check ACF/PACF before choosing p and q.
    • Where these models earn their keep: volatility (it has memory), cointegrated spreads, macro series. "Time-series models aren't a way to predict tomorrow's price."

    Volatility has memory: EWMA and GARCH#

    GARCH(1,1): σ²_t = ω + α·ε²_(t−1) + β·σ²_(t−1)persistence = α + β annualise volatility × √252 (not × 252)
    • EWMA weights recent squared returns more, with one decay parameter and nothing to fit. GARCH adds a long-run anchor ω: α is the reaction to yesterday's shock, β the memory.
    • NIFTY since 2021: α ≈ 0.103, β ≈ 0.872, persistence ≈ 0.975 (our arithmetic: a shock's half-life ≈ 27 trading days). Next-day forecast 0.83% ≈ 13.2% annualised; history ranged 9–37%.
    • Multi-step forecasts drift back to the long-run level. Use them to size (smaller when volatility is high), price options, run VaR and stress tests, and flag regime shifts. Compare with India VIX, the market's implied view.
    • Scale returns to percent and drop the first NaN before fitting.

    The alpha research process#

    1. Hypothesis with a number, universe and horizon: "NSE stocks in the top 12-month return quintile beat the bottom quintile next month, after costs."
    2. Fair test: no look-ahead, no survivorship, realistic costs; fix the success metric before looking.
    3. Split: search in-sample, test the final idea once out of sample.
    4. Economic reason: who pays the edge, and why? "It works but I don't know why" is a warning.
    5. Decay and capacity: crowding weakens edges; impact caps the money they can hold.

    NIFTY "long above SMA(n)", searched over n = 10…120: best in-sample SMA 35 with Sharpe 1.23, the same rule out of sample 0.57. Most of the in-sample shine was fitting the past. The process exists to kill ideas; trade what survives.

    Flow-based strategies: FII/DII, OI and expiry#

    • Price + OI quadrants: long and short build-ups are fresh money (fuel); covering and unwinding are exhaustion. RELIANCE Jul future, 37 sessions: 14 long and 22 short build-up days (a young contract only shows build-ups; use the front month or all expiries for unwinds).

    • Ban list: OI ≥ 95% of MWPL bans new positions next day, lifting below 80%: a crowding and liquidity warning, not a directional bet.
    • Expiry sets the clock: pinning toward heavy-OI strikes, and rollover (RELIANCE's next-month OI +823% into June expiry). High rollover suggests conviction; roll cost is part of carry.
    • Score each flow as a signed input and act only on confluence that price confirms.

    Backtesting without fooling yourself#

    • A backtest is a measuring instrument that can be miscalibrated: data → signal → fills → costs → P&L → metrics, and one leak anywhere makes the metrics fiction.
    • Fills: never at the close that generated the signal; default to the next bar's open, pay at least half the spread, and model unfilled limit orders.
    • Costs: STT, stamp, exchange, SEBI, GST, brokerage plus spread and impact. "A frictionless backtest is a fantasy."
    • One missing .shift(1): NIFTY 20-day SMA sign rule shows Sharpe +4.51 and 25.2× with look-ahead, +0.16 and 1.07× lagged.
    • Red flags: too-good Sharpe, a curve without real drawdowns. Sharpe 2 after 1,000 trials is worth less than Sharpe 1 on the first honest try. Try to disprove the strategy.

    Portfolio construction, VaR and stress#

    inverse-vol (risk parity) weight: w_i = (1/σ_i) / Σ(1/σ_j)Kelly fraction ≈ edge / variance → bet half or a quarter of itfactor risk model: Σ ≈ B·F·Bᵀ + DVaR: loss not exceeded at a confidence · CVaR (expected shortfall): average loss beyond VaR
    • Portfolio risk depends on covariance, not the average of individual risks; diversification is "the only free lunch". Five NSE stocks (RELIANCE 21%, HDFCBANK 22%, INFY 17%, ITC 23%, TATASTEEL 16%): volatility 14.1%, below every member's. One-day 95% VaR −1.42%, CVaR −2.04%, worst day −5.24%.
    • Raw Markowitz amplifies noisy return guesses. Robustness order: equal weight → risk parity → shrunk, constrained mean-variance (Ledoit–Wolf, caps, Black–Litterman) → raw Markowitz.
    • Volatility targeting (e.g. 12% a year) scales exposure inversely to realised volatility. Full Kelly on an overestimated edge is really 2–3× Kelly: growth falls and ruin follows.

    Research to production: one signal, two callers#

    • The research-production gap comes from re-implementing the signal, different warm-up, hand-typed parameters, bar-open vs bar-close timestamps, full-series vs last-bar maths.

    Monitoring, reconciliation and incidents#

    • Live monitor every few seconds: net P&L (realised + MTM), positions, exposure vs limits, fills and rejects, feed staleness, strategy heartbeat, shown beside the broker's view. Divergence is the danger signal.
    • "A limit you do not alert on is a comment, not a control." RELIANCE long 500 with a −₹2,500 day limit: breached at 14:40 (low −₹2,800) → alert and halt new orders.
    • Reconcile trades, positions and cash: intraday every few minutes, end of day against contract notes. A break example: internal +1,000 shares vs broker +1,500 (₹6.6 lakh): one fill lost by the feed handler.
    1. Stabilise: halt; flatten or hedge to the broker's position
    2. Diagnose: rebuild the timeline from logs
    3. Fix the root cause
    4. Resume only after a clean reconciliation

    Blameless post-mortems end in a specific new control, never "be more careful". Every good control is a scar from a past incident.

    SEBI compliance, ethics and the quant career#

    • SEBI governs algo trading: algos route through registered brokers with approvals, unique strategy IDs and mandatory controls (price and quantity limits, kill switches). The retail framework keeps evolving: read the latest circulars.
    • Red lines: spoofing, layering, pump-and-dump, front-running, and reckless strategies that destabilise thin stocks. "I can" is not "I should".
    • Homes: sell-side, buy-side, prop, HFT. Roles: researcher, developer, trader. Interview pillars: probability puzzles, coding, statistics and ML, markets and products.
    • Puzzle habit: break a stick at two random points; the chance the pieces form a triangle is 1/4, and 500,000 simulations give ≈ 0.2497. Expected flips until two heads in a row: 6. When maths is hard, simulate; then verify.
    • Break in with an honest strategy, a clean out-of-sample record, sandbox history and open-source work: intellectual honesty is the credential.

    10Checklists and quick reference

    The whole course compressed into checks you can run.

    Research checklist#

    1. Hypothesis is precise and falsifiable, with an economic reason (who pays, and why).
    2. Data is point-in-time, survivorship-free and corporate-action adjusted.
    3. Every signal is lagged; fills at the next open, paying at least half the spread.
    4. Full Indian cost stack plus impact, sized at the capital you intend to run.
    5. Every trial logged; Sharpe deflated or corrected (Holm / BH) for the search.
    6. Purged walk-forward, judged on the whole fold distribution vs a benchmark.
    7. Enough trades; a curve with real drawdowns; capacity and half-life estimated.

    Production checklist#

    1. One shared signal module and one config; a daily parity test.
    2. Pre-trade gate: latched kill switch, rupee loss and position limits, rate throttle.
    3. Unique client order IDs; query on timeout, never resend blindly.
    4. Append-only event log; replay then reconcile with the broker on restart.
    5. Live monitor with alerts wired to every limit; intraday and end-of-day reconciliation.
    6. Registered algo ID, static IP, guarded API key; OTR within budget.
    7. Sandbox → paper-to-small → full size, with versioned rollback.
