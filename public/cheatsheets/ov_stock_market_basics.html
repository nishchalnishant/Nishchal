    01Money and markets: why invest, where, and what a share is

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Stock Market Basics · chapters 1–3 (why invest, asset classes, what a stock is)

    Inflation and real returns#

    • Inflation quietly shrinks what each rupee buys. At about 6% a year, ₹1 lakh kept idle from 2000 buys only what about ₹23,000 bought then, roughly 25 years later.
    • Saving protects the rupee amount; investing aims to protect and grow buying power. You need both: a cash cushion and growing assets.
    • A savings account at about 3% against 5–6% inflation is a slow leak.
    real return ≈ nominal return − inflationFD 7% − inflation 6% ≈ 1% realequity ~12% − inflation 6% ≈ 6% real

    Always judge returns after inflation (and tax), never by the headline number.

    Compounding and starting early#

    ₹5,000/month SIP at ~12%InvestedApprox. value
    10 years₹6 lakh₹11.6 lakh
    20 years₹12 lakh₹50 lakh
    30 years₹18 lakh₹1.76 crore
    • Tripling the time multiplied the result about 15 times: the last years do the most work.
    • Start early: someone investing ₹5,000 a month from 25 to 35 and then stopping ends at 60 with more than someone investing the same from 35 to 60, despite putting in less than half.
    • Time in the market beats timing the market.

    Funds (mutual funds, ETFs, index funds) are baskets of these building blocks. Higher potential return always comes with higher risk; risk means a wider range of outcomes, not certain loss. Tax rates change with each Budget, so check the current ones.

    Asset allocation basics#

    • Spread money across classes so one can offset another.
    • Rough starting rule: equity share ≈ 100 − age (a 25-year-old ≈ 75% equity, the rest debt and a little gold). A starting point, not a law.
    • Emergency fund in cash or liquid funds; never in property.
    • Money needed within 3–5 years stays out of equities.
    • Equity has built the most long-run wealth because it owns growing businesses that reinvest profits: the Sensex went from a base of 100 (1979) to roughly 76,000, about 770× in ~45 years.

    Recent asset-class notes#

    • Gold: rose ~27% in 2024 and surged further in 2025 (international gold above $4,000/oz). A strong run does not guarantee a repeat.
    • Sovereign Gold Bonds: no new tranches after the 2025 Budget; existing bonds keep paying 2.5% a year to maturity. New buyers use gold ETFs or funds.
    • Crypto: can swing 10% in a day and has fallen 70%+ from peaks in past cycles; it pays no profits or dividends. Never the foundation of wealth.

    What a share actually is#

    • A share is legal part-ownership of a company. Your stake = your shares ÷ shares outstanding.
    • Rights: a claim on profits (dividends), a claim on what is left after debts, and usually one vote per share at the AGM.
    • You earn two ways: price appreciation (capital gains) and dividends while you hold.
    • Price discovery: a continuous auction. More eager buyers than sellers lifts the price; the displayed price is just the latest agreed trade.
    • Per-share price says nothing about value: MRF trades above ₹1.5 lakh a share simply because it has never split its shares.

    02The market ecosystem: regulators, plumbing and settlement

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Stock Market Basics · chapters 4–6 (SEBI and regulators, exchanges, brokers, depositories, clearing and settlement)

    SEBI and RBI: who guards what#

    RegulatorWatchesMain jobs
    SEBIShares, bonds, mutual funds, derivativesProtect investors; register and regulate exchanges, brokers, funds, advisers; develop the market (faster settlement, demat, disclosure)
    RBIThe rupee and banksIssue currency, fight inflation through the repo rate, regulate banks, manage forex
    • Investor protection toolkit: bans on insider trading, manipulation and misleading ads; mandatory disclosure; shares held in your own demat account; fines and bans; the SCORES complaint portal.
    • SEBI registration signals accountability, not guaranteed profits.

    Reforms usually follow scandals#

    YearEventOutcome
    1988SEBI formedAdvisory body, few powers
    1992Harshad Mehta scamSEBI Act gives statutory powers
    2001Ketan Parekh scamTighter settlement; badla banned

    2024–25Finfluencer rulesRegistered entities can't tie up with unregistered finfluencers
    FY25SEBI F&O study~91% of individual F&O traders lost money; fewer weekly expiries, bigger lot sizes

    Exchanges, brokers, depositories, clearing#

    PlayerJobIndia
    ExchangeMatches orders and publishes trades; does not set pricesBSE (1875, Asia's oldest), NSE (1994, largest by volume)
    BrokerLicensed member that routes your ordersDiscount or full-service
    DepositoryHolds your shares electronicallyNSDL (1996), CDSL
    DPYour broker as the link to the depositoryCharges ₹13–20 per sell leaving demat
    Clearing corporationGuarantees both sides of every tradeNSE Clearing, Indian Clearing Corporation

    Exchanges match, brokers route, depositories hold, clearing corporations guarantee. Your shares sit at NSDL or CDSL, not with your broker.

    Trading hours and the closing auction#

    Session (IST, Mon–Fri)Time
    Pre-open (call auction sets the open)9:00–9:15
    Normal market9:15–15:30
    Post-close (orders at the closing price)15:40–16:00
    • Orders placed while closed wait; an AMO (after-market order) is sent at the next open. Diwali has a one-hour Muhurat session.
    • Closing auction session (from 3 Aug 2026) for stocks with derivatives: continuous trading stops at 15:15; the reference price is the 15:00–15:15 VWAP; order entry closes at a random moment between 15:28 and 15:30; matching ends by 15:35, within ±3% of the reference. Other stocks keep the last-30-minute VWAP close.
    • Equity derivatives are not in the auction: they trade to 15:40 and close on the 15:10–15:40 VWAP.

    Brokers and the true cost of a trade#

    Discount brokerFull-service broker
    OffersPlatform onlyTrades plus research and advice
    BrokerageFlat, about ₹20 per order; often zero on deliveryUsually a % of trade value
    SuitsSelf-directed investorsInvestors who want guidance
    • Every trade also pays STT, exchange charges, 18% GST on brokerage and charges, stamp duty (buys), a SEBI fee, and DP charges on sells.
    • "Zero brokerage" is not zero cost: on tiny trades the statutory charges can exceed brokerage.

    Demat account types#

    • Regular (residents), joint, corporate, custodial (minors via a guardian), premium (frequent traders).
    • NRI: repatriable (linked to an NRE account) and non-repatriable (NRO account).
    • BSDA (basic services demat): no annual charge up to ₹4 lakh of holdings, a small fee up to ₹10 lakh; only allowed as your single demat account.
    • India passed about 21.6 crore demat accounts by December 2025 (CDSL ~17 crore, NSDL ~4 crore but more by value); far fewer are active in a year.

    From buy click to shares in demat#

    flowchart TD
  O["You place an order"] --> B["Broker routes it"]
  B --> X["Exchange matches a seller"]
  X --> C["Clearing corporation becomes the counterparty"]
  C --> N["Netting: one net obligation per security"]
  N --> S["Settlement T+1: pay-in and pay-out"]
  S --> D["Shares land in your demat"]

    A trade is the matched promise; clearing works out who owes what; settlement moves the money and shares, both legs at once so nobody has to trust the other side.

    Settlement cycles and T+0#

    PeriodCycle
    Until 2001Weekly badla settlement
    2002T+3
    2003T+2 (the global norm for two decades)
    January 2023T+1, fully adopted (the US followed in May 2024)
    2024–25Optional T+0 beta, phased across the top 500 stocks about 100 at a time
    • T+1: sale proceeds usable and purchases deliverable the next working day.
    • T+0 runs alongside T+1 and is optional. It is about when ownership finalises, not intraday trading.

    Why trades almost never fail#

    1. Novation: the clearing corporation becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer, removing counterparty risk.
    2. Margins: traders deposit money upfront as a cushion.
    3. Settlement Guarantee Fund: a pool that completes settlement if a member defaults.
    4. Auctions and penalties: if a seller fails to deliver, shares are bought in the market for the buyer and the defaulter pays.

    Netting example: buy 10 and sell 5 of the same stock in a day, and only the net 5 shares move.

    03Going public: funding, IPOs and corporate actions

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Stock Market Basics · chapters 7–9 (how companies get funded, the IPO, rights, FPO, OFS, bonus and splits)

    The funding ladder#

    flowchart TD
  I["Idea"] --> BS["Bootstrapping"]
  BS --> AN["Friends, family, angels"]
  AN --> VC["Venture capital: seed, Series A, B, C"]
  VC --> PE["Private equity"]
  PE --> IPO["IPO and listing"]
    • Each rung usually brings bigger cheques at higher valuations: angels ₹25 lakh to a few crore, VC rounds crores to hundreds of crore, PE hundreds to thousands of crore.
    • Unicorn: a startup valued at $1 billion or more; India has well over 100.

    Valuation and dilution#

    implied valuation = investment ÷ stake acquired₹10 crore for 20% → ₹10 ÷ 0.20 = ₹50 crorefounder's stake value = valuation × founder %
    StageValuationFounder %Stake value
    Bootstrapped₹2 cr100%₹2 cr
    Seed₹20 cr75%₹15 cr
    Series A₹100 cr55%₹55 cr
    Series B₹500 cr40%₹200 cr
    IPO₹4,000 cr30%₹1,200 cr

    A smaller slice of a much bigger company can be worth far more. Private valuations are negotiated opinions, not guarantees of IPO returns.

    Why companies go public#

    • Capital: raise hundreds to thousands of crore for growth or to repay debt.
    • Liquidity: early investors and employees can finally sell.
    • Visibility: trust from customers, lenders and hires.
    • Cost: quarterly disclosure, public scrutiny, and a share price judged every day.

    How an IPO works#

    flowchart LR
  D1["DRHP filed with SEBI"] --> D2["RHP with price band and dates"]
  D2 --> D3["Book-building: about 3 days of bids"]
  D3 --> D4["Cut-off price fixed"]
  D4 --> D5["Allotment: lottery if oversubscribed"]
  D5 --> D6["Unallotted money unblocked"]
  D5 --> D7["Listing day: trading starts"]
    • Fresh issue = new shares, money to the company. OFS = existing holders sell, money to them. Check the mix in the offer document.
    • Apply in lots through ASBA/UPI: the money is blocked, not debited, until allotment.
    • Retail allotment is a random draw of one lot per applicant, so applying for more lots does not raise your odds of the first lot.
    • GMP (grey market premium) is unofficial and unregulated rumour, not a reason to apply.

    IPO categories and what to read#

    CategoryWhoShare of issue
    QIBMutual funds, banks, insurers, FPIsUp to 50%
    NII / HNIIndividuals above ₹2 lakhAt least 15%
    RetailUp to ₹2 lakhAt least 35%
    • Anchors take up to 60% of the QIB part a day early; half locked in for 30 days, half for 90.
    • In the RHP, read the risk factors first, then objects of the issue, financials, promoter holding and the basis for the price versus listed peers.
    • SME IPOs (NSE Emerge, BSE SME) have lighter rules, higher minimums and much higher volatility: not for first-timers.

    Listing day is not a promise#

    listing gain % = (listing price − issue price) ÷ issue price × 100(150 − 70) ÷ 70 ≈ +114%
    • Big brands can list flat or lower: India's largest IPO to date listed about 1% below its issue price.
    • Big listing pops can fade: one 2024 issue more than doubled on day one, then fell about a quarter over the following months.
    • 2024 saw about 91 mainboard IPOs raising roughly ₹1.6 lakh crore.

    Corporate actions at a glance#

    ActionNew shares?Money goes toPrice effectYour wealth on the day
    Rights issueYes, if you subscribeCompanyAdjusts down slightlyNeutral if you subscribe or sell the entitlement
    FPOYesCompanyCan dip on dilutionDiluted unless you buy
    OFSNoSelling holdersShort-term pressureNo dilution
    DividendNoShareholders (cash)Drops by the dividend on ex-dateNeutral

    Since 1 October 2024, buyback proceeds are taxed as dividend income in shareholders' hands.

    Adjustment arithmetic#

    1:1 bonus: new price = old price ÷ 2split: new price = old price ÷ split ratioex-dividend open ≈ previous close − dividend
    • Bonus: 10 shares × ₹2,900 = ₹29,000 becomes 20 × ₹1,450 = ₹29,000.
    • Split ₹10 face value to ₹1: one share becomes ten at a tenth of the price.
    • Rights 1:5 at ₹200 when the market is ₹300: the entitlement has value because of the ₹100 discount; subscribe or sell it, never ignore it.
    • Under T+1 the ex-date and record date usually coincide: own the share before the ex-date to get the dividend.

    Anatomy of a stock quote#

    FieldMeaning
    LTPPrice of the most recent trade (a record, not a forecast)
    Previous closeYesterday's official close; the reference for "change"
    ChangeLTP − previous close, in ₹ and %
    Bid / askBest price a buyer will pay / a seller will accept right now
    SpreadAsk − bid: paise means liquid, rupees means thin
    OHLCOpen, high, low, close: one candlestick
    VolumeShares traded so far in the session
    change % = (LTP − previous close) ÷ previous close × 100(1,476.30 − 1,468.50) ÷ 1,468.50 ≈ +0.53%

    Market depth: the five best bids and asks#

    Bid qty   Bid      Ask      Ask qty
   320  1,476.20  1,476.30     280
   540  1,476.10  1,476.40     610
 1,250  1,476.00  1,476.50     900
   410  1,475.90  1,476.60     350

Buy 3,280 vs sell 3,240 → roughly balanced
    • Left side is demand below the price; right side is supply above it.
    • Depth is a snapshot, not a crystal ball: large orders can be cancelled in an instant.
    • Price up on high volume shows conviction; up on thin volume is tentative.

    Circuits: daily price bands#

    • Individual stocks have bands of 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%; large derivative stocks have wider, flexible bands and rarely lock.
    • Upper circuit: only buyers left, trading freezes. Lower circuit: only sellers, and holders may not be able to exit for days.
    • Market-wide breakers: a 10%, 15% or 20% fall in the Nifty or Sensex pauses all trading.
    • Circuits trap both sides: chasing an upper-circuit stock can leave you unable to sell when it turns.

    How an index is built#

    • An index is one number for a weighted basket of stocks. You can't buy it, but an index fund or ETF holds every constituent at its weight.
    • Weight comes from free-float market cap: only shares available to the public count, not promoter or government holdings.
    • Nifty 50 (NSE, 50 stocks) and Sensex (BSE, 30 stocks) overlap heavily and usually move by similar percentages.
    • Weights drift daily and constituents are reviewed twice a year.
    stock's contribution = weight × % moveindex move = sum of all contributions10.6% weight × 2% move ≈ +0.21% on the index0.3% weight × 2% move ≈ +0.006%

    The Nifty is top-heavy#

    CompanySectorWeight (approx.)
    HDFC BankBanking10.6%
    ICICI BankBanking8.3%
    Reliance IndustriesEnergy, retail, telecom8.3%
    Bharti AirtelTelecom5.2%

    • "The market fell" often means a few heavyweights fell: check breadth before concluding.
    • Sector indices (Bank Nifty, Nifty IT, Auto, Pharma, FMCG, Metal) show what is driving the day; Nifty 100, 500, midcap and smallcap indices show the wider market.

    Bull, bear and size buckets#

    • Bull market: a sustained rise. Bear market: a fall of 20% or more from a recent peak. Correction: about 10% or more. Crash: a sudden, panic-driven plunge.
    • Breadth: advancing versus declining stocks; weak breadth means a few giants are hiding a falling market.
    • Volatility: the size and speed of swings in either direction.
    BucketSEBI rank by market capCharacter
    Large-capTop 100Steadier, lower risk
    Mid-cap101–250Growth with more risk
    Small-cap251 and belowBiggest swings both ways

    A penny stock's low price is not a bargain; liquidity matters most on the day you need to exit.

    Long vs short#

    • Long: buy now, sell later; profit if the price rises; the worst case is losing what you paid.
    • Short: sell first, buy back later; profit if the price falls; the loss is unlimited because prices have no ceiling.
    • In India most retail shorts are intraday only; holding a short overnight needs formal borrowing through SLB (securities lending and borrowing).
    short profit per share = sell price − buy-back pricesell 100, buy back 80 → ₹20

    05What moves prices: expectations, macro and events

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Stock Market Basics · chapters 13–15 (what moves a stock, macro forces, events and prices)

    Prices move on surprise, not on news#

    • Price is the latest agreed trade, not fair value. Every tick is an imbalance between eager buyers and eager sellers.
    • What tips the balance: news, earnings, expectations and market-wide sentiment.
    • Markets price the future: results are judged against expectations. A stock expected to grow profit 15% that reports 12% can fall, even though the business grew.
    • Buy the rumour, sell the news: prices often run up before good news and slip once it arrives.
    Result vs expectationTypical reaction
    BeatOften jumps
    MeetUsually muted
    MissOften falls

    Measuring returns properly#

    absolute return = selling price − buying price% return = gain ÷ buying price × 100₹100 gain on ₹400 = 25%; the same ₹100 on ₹2,000 = 5%
    • Always state the time: doubling in 15 years is under 5% a year, below a typical FD.
    • Use annualised returns to compare holdings with different periods.

    Rates, inflation and the RBI#

    • The repo rate is the price of money. Lower rates mean cheaper loans, more spending, money leaving FDs for equities, and a higher present value of future profits: generally supportive for stocks. Higher rates do the reverse.
    • The RBI targets about 4% inflation within a 2–6% band. Hot inflation brings hikes; inflation well inside the band leaves room to cut.
    • 2025 example (approx.): repo cut from about 6.5% to about 5.25% as retail inflation fell sharply.

    FIIs vs DIIs#

    FIIs / FPIsDIIs
    WhoForeign funds and pensionsMutual funds, insurers, pension funds
    MoneyGlobal capitalDomestic savings and SIPs
    BehaviourLarge, fast, driven by global cuesSteadier, fed every month by SIPs
    When they sellIndex can drop sharplyOften cushions the fall

    In 2025 record FII selling (about ₹1.66 lakh crore) was largely absorbed by domestic SIP inflows of about ₹31,000 crore a month; DII ownership of NSE-listed companies overtook FII ownership.

    Crude, rupee and inflation chain#

    flowchart TD
  C["Crude oil rises"] --> M["Bigger import bill: India imports about 85% of its crude"]
  M --> U["More dollar demand, rupee weakens"]
  U --> P["Imports cost more, inflation rises"]
  P --> R["RBI keeps rates higher"]
  R --> S["Headwind for stocks"]

    A weaker rupee hurts importers (oil marketers, many manufacturers) and often helps dollar earners such as IT services and pharma.

    Global cues and the macro mood check#

    • A hawkish US Fed tends to pull money towards the US and away from emerging markets like India.
    • Overnight US and Asian markets and GIFT Nifty hint at India's open; wars, oil shocks, elections and trade news spread within hours.
    1. Are rates rising or falling?
    2. Is inflation under control?
    3. Is the rupee stable or sliding?
    4. Is crude calm or spiking?
    5. Are FIIs net sellers or buyers?
    6. Is the world risk-on or risk-off?

    Macro is context, not a timing tool: check it at turning points, then stick to your plan.

    Results season and dividend dates#

    • Listed companies report four times a year: Q1 Apr–Jun, Q2 Jul–Sep, Q3 Oct–Dec, Q4 Jan–Mar; each season lasts about six weeks.
    • Watch revenue (top line), net profit (bottom line), margins, growth year on year and quarter on quarter, and above all guidance: a guidance cut can sink a stock despite good results.
    • Dividend dates: announcement, then ex-date (own it before this), then record date.
    dividend yield = annual dividend ÷ share price × 100₹20 on a ₹400 stock = 5%

    Catalysts to plan around#

    TypeExamples
    Company-specificMergers and acquisitions, regulatory news, management changes, big orders, court rulings, rating changes, index inclusion (index funds must buy) or exclusion
    Scheduled, market-wideUnion Budget (1 February), election results, RBI policy
    Unscheduled shocksPandemics, wars, banking scares, surprise central-bank moves

    Results-day prices can whip both ways within minutes: learn from the calendar, don't trade the noise.

    06Becoming an investor: first steps, mindset and staying safe

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Stock Market Basics · chapters 16–18 (first steps, risk and mindset, scams and protection)

    Opening your accounts#

    • You need three linked accounts: bank (money), trading (places orders) and demat (holds shares). Trading and demat are usually opened together online in a day or two.
    • KYC documents: PAN, Aadhaar (OTP e-verification), bank details, photo and signature, sometimes a video check; then e-sign.
    • Opening an account is the smallest milestone. Being ready means an emergency fund, no costly debt, a simple plan and a steady SIP.

    Index SIP or direct stocks?#

    Index fund SIPDirect stocks
    You ownA slice of the whole indexCompanies you pick
    DiversificationInstantBuild it yourself
    EffortLow, automatedHigh, research each one
    RiskSpread outConcentrated
    Best forMost beginnersLater, once you can justify each holding

    SIPs bring automatic discipline and rupee-cost averaging: a fixed amount buys more units when prices are low. Many start from ₹500 a month.

    Beginner roadmap#

    1. Emergency fund of 3–6 months' expenses in a safe, liquid place.
    2. Clear costly debt such as credit cards and expensive personal loans.
    3. Open demat and trading accounts; complete KYC.
    4. Start a small automated SIP in a broad index fund (₹500–1,000 a month).
    5. Keep learning about what you own.
    6. Add direct stocks slowly, with a small slice of money you can afford to lose.
    7. Stay consistent; let compounding work for years.

    Before buying any single stock#

    1. Can I explain what the business does and how it makes money?
    2. Why could it be bigger or more profitable in 5–10 years?
    3. What could break that story?
    4. How much of my portfolio will it be, and what will I do if it falls 50%?

    Borrowed conviction from tips and TV leads to panic-selling at the first dip. Form your own view or stay with the index.

    Position sizing: why one stock can't sink you#

    portfolio hit = weight × stock's fall5% weight × 50% fall = 2.5% of wealth80% weight × 50% fall = 40% of wealth
    • Higher return always means higher risk; a "guaranteed high return" is a misunderstanding or a scam.
    • Expect markets to fall 30–50% now and then. That is normal, not a malfunction.

    BiasLooks likeCounter

    Studies such as Morningstar's "Mind the Gap" find the average fund investor earns roughly 1–1.5 points a year less than their own funds because of badly timed buying and selling. Habits that help: automate, write down why you own each holding, review monthly or quarterly, and do nothing for a day after a big win or loss.

    Modern traps#

    • Pump and dump: operators buy a cheap illiquid stock, hype it in WhatsApp or Telegram groups, then sell into the buying they created.
    • Finfluencers and fake advisers: paid tips without SEBI registration are illegal; registered firms may not tie up with unregistered finfluencers.
    • Guaranteed-return apps: early withdrawals work, new deposits pay old ones, then the app vanishes.
    • Dabba trading: "trades" that never reach an exchange; a criminal offence with no legal protection for you.

    Know the two registrations: a Research Analyst publishes research views; a Registered Investment Adviser gives personal, fee-based advice with a duty to you.

    If you have been defrauded#

    1. Stop all further payments immediately.
    2. Save screenshots, chats and transaction records.

    07Checklists and one-line formulas

    Run these before your first investment, before any single stock, and whenever a tip arrives.

    Before you start investing#

    • Emergency fund of 3–6 months in place.
    • No credit-card or expensive personal-loan debt.
    • Demat, trading and bank accounts linked; KYC done.
    • A monthly SIP amount you can keep paying in bad years.
    • Money needed within 3–5 years kept out of equities.
    • Returns judged after inflation and tax.

    Before buying a stock#

    • I can explain the business and why it grows.
    • I know what is already expected and priced in.
    • The position size survives a 50% fall.
    • I checked the spread, volume and circuit band.
    • I checked the corporate-action calendar.
    • My sell rules are written down.

    Staying safe#

    • Deal only with SEBI-registered brokers, advisers and funds; verify on sebi.gov.in.
    • Never share passwords, OTPs or remote access.
    • Ignore "sure-shot" tips and guaranteed returns.
    • Keep shares in your own demat; read your contract notes.
    • Ask who gains if you buy.
