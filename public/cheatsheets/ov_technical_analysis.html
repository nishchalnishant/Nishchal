    01Getting started: what TA is, charts, candles and timeframes

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 1–4 (what TA is, reading charts, candlestick anatomy, timeframes and scales)

    What technical analysis is, and isn't#

    • The study of price and volume to tilt the odds and limit losses: probability, not prophecy.
    • Three working assumptions (tendencies, not laws): price reflects known information; trends persist until something real changes; crowd behaviour repeats, so patterns recur, though never identically.
    • Charts can show trend and its health, mark levels where the crowd reacted before, and show where your idea is wrong. They can't predict tomorrow, guarantee a pattern, or rescue a position with no exit plan.
    • The real edge: predefined exits, repeatable rules instead of moods, and avoiding falling knives.

    Technical vs fundamental#

    FundamentalTechnical
    QuestionWhat is it worth?What is price doing, and will it continue?
    StudiesThe companyPrice and volume
    HorizonMonths to years: what to ownDays to weeks: when to act

    A common pairing: fundamentals build the shortlist, technicals time the entry and exit. Always ask "compared to what?" and test popular claims instead of believing them.

    Chart types#

    StyleShowsStrengthWeakness
    LineCloses onlyClear directionHides the path within each bar
    Bar (OHLC)Open, high, low, closeComplete, compactSlower to scan
    CandlestickOHLC plus colour and bodyBuyer–seller balance at a glanceBusy on dense charts

    Before forming any view, check three things: which stock, what one bar represents, and the price range from top to bottom. A squeezed axis can make a 1% move look violent.

    Reading a single candle#

    1. Colour: close above open (green) or below (red) shows who won that bar. It compares open and close of the same bar, so a gap-down day can still be green.
    2. Body length: how decisive the win was.
    3. Wicks: ground traded but not held. A long lower wick means buyers defended lower prices; a long upper wick, sellers defended higher ones.
    4. Size: judge it against recent bars, never in absolute terms.
    5. Context: trend, level and volume decide whether it means anything.

    A marubozu (all body) is one-sided conviction; a doji (almost no body) is balance. One candle is a whisper, not a signal.

    Timeframes: direction vs timing#

    Holding periodTrade onCheck direction on
    IntradayMinutesDaily
    Swing (days)DailyWeekly
    Long termWeekly or monthlyMonthly
    • Decide the holding period first, then the timeframe, never the reverse.
    • The higher timeframe sets direction; the lower sets timing. A dip on the hourly inside a daily uptrend is usually just a pullback.
    • The course uses daily charts: one decision a day, enough signals to measure, less order-matching noise.

    Scales and chart settings that mislead#

    • Linear scale: equal rupee moves take equal height. Log scale: equal percentage moves take equal height. 100→200 and 1,000→2,000 look the same on log (both +100%).
    • Use log for long, large-percentage moves and linear for short, tight ranges.
    • Truncated axis and auto-scaling exaggerate or shrink identical candles; read the axis numbers first.
    • Date window: start at a crash low and anything looks heroic; start at a peak and it looks broken.
    • Aggregation: where a week or month is cut can flip a weekly candle's colour.

    02Structure and levels: Dow theory, trends, support and resistance, breakouts, Fibonacci

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 5–9 (Dow theory, trends and trendlines, support and resistance, channels and breakouts, Fibonacci)

    Dow theory: six tenets and three trends#

    1. Prices already reflect known information.
    2. Three trends run at once.
    3. Primary trends move through phases.
    4. The averages must confirm each other.
    5. Volume must confirm the trend.
    6. A trend holds until a clear reversal.
    TrendLastsRole
    PrimaryMonths to yearsBuilds or destroys wealth
    SecondaryWeeksRetraces about a third to two-thirds of the prior move
    MinorDaysNoise; timing only

    Market cycle and the volume rule#

    flowchart TD
  AC["Accumulation: quiet base"] --> MU["Markup: higher highs and lows"]
  MU --> DI["Distribution: churning top"]
  DI --> MD["Markdown: lower highs and lows"]
  MD --> AC
    • Volume should grow with the main trend and fade on counter-moves; thin rallies or heavy declines are warnings, not reversal triggers.
    • Phases are clearest in hindsight; don't label them in real time.

    A Dow trend filter vs buy-and-hold#

    Rule tested on 48 Nifty 50 stocks over 16 years: hold only while price is above the 200-day average and the 50-day is above the 200-day.

    Trend filterBuy and hold
    Median return~107%~744%
    Median max drawdown~47%~56%
    Beat buy-and-hold4 of 48 stocks—
    Time out of the market~45%0%

    Dow-style filters are a risk-management framework, not a profit machine: they trimmed drawdowns but gave up most of the return.

    Trends and trendlines#

    • Name the state first: up (higher highs and lows: buy dips), down (lower highs and lows: don't buy weakness), or sideways (buy the floor, sell the ceiling).
    • Trendlines: join rising swing lows (uptrend) or falling swing highs (downtrend). Two points draw it, a third confirms; 3–4 clean touches is ideal, eight means it's tired. Never force or keep redrawing it.
    • Angle: a gentle slope can last months; near-vertical is exhaustion risk, not strength. Use a log scale on long charts.
    • A trendline break means the angle changed; a broken swing low means the trend changed. Decide on swing points, not lines.
    • Dip into a rising 50-day average: +0.67% over the next 10 days, against +0.58% for any uptrend day. Real but tiny.

    Support and resistance#

    • Why levels work: traders who profited there buy more, traders who missed it buy the return, and trapped holders sell at breakeven.
    • Where levels come from: prior swing highs and lows (most important), round numbers like 500 or 1,000, and long sideways bases.
    • Draw zones, not lines: mark the cluster of highs or lows, not the single extreme tick. A practical start: the highest high and lowest low of the last 20 sessions.
    • Role reversal: broken resistance tends to become support and vice versa; the retest is usually the cleaner entry.
    • Many tests weaken a level: each test absorbs orders. A level hit 13–14 times is a countdown to a break, not a stronger wall.
    • Confluence (two independent reasons at one price) makes a zone stronger.

    Ranges, channels and breakouts#

    • A range is a sideways box; a channel is the same idea with parallel sloping lines. Markets spend roughly two-thirds of their time sideways.
    • Inside a range: buy near support, sell near resistance, avoid the middle, and put the stop just outside the box.
    • A close above the upper Bollinger band still averaged about +0.58% over 10 days: touching an edge is not automatically a reversal.
    Breakout test (new 20-day high)Fakeout rate
    All breakouts56% back inside within 5 sessions
    On volume above 1.5× average47%

    Treat fresh breakouts as guilty until proven innocent: wait for a close beyond the level, then the retest.

    The retest filters out many failed breakouts and gives a tighter stop than chasing the first breakout candle.

    Fibonacci: the pullback map#

    LevelReading
    23.6%Shallow dip in a strong trend
    38.2%First level most traders watch
    50%Not Fibonacci; Dow's halfway level
    61.8%Deepest normal pullback in a healthy trend
    78.6%Last-ditch; beyond it the move is usually broken
    127.2%, 161.8%, 261.8%Extension zones for taking profit
    level = swing high − ratio × (swing high − swing low) (uptrend)extension target = pullback low + ratio × original move

    Fibonacci worked example and rules#

    • Swing 553.5 → 1,094.2 (move ≈ 540): 38.2% at 887.7, 50% at 823.9, 61.8% at 760.0. The pullback bottomed near 788, about 57%, inside the 50–61.8% decision zone, then resumed up.
    • Into the zone with a turn signal (hammer, engulfing, rising volume): healthy trend. Slicing through 61.8% and still falling: trend may be over.
    • Pick the swing points first and let the levels fall where they fall; drawing after deciding to buy is self-deception.
    • Confluence that strengthens a level: prior support, a rising 50-day average, a round number, the same level on the weekly chart.

    03Candlestick patterns and why context decides

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 10–12 (single candles, two- and three-candle tells, patterns in context)

    Single candles#

    CandleShapeWhere it countsReading
    DojiOpen ≈ close; body under about a tenth of the rangeAfter a long runMomentum pause, not a reversal call
    Gravestone / dragonfly dojiLong upper / long lower wickAt resistance / supportBuying / selling rejected
    Spinning topSmall body (under a third of the range), wicks both sidesAfter a strong trendNeither side wins; fuel running low
    HammerSmall body on top, lower wick ≥ 2× bodyAfter a decline, at supportBullish hint
    Hanging manSame shape as a hammerAfter a rally, at resistanceBearish warning
    Shooting starSmall body at bottom, long upper wickAfter a rally, at resistanceBearish hint
    Inverted hammerSame shape as a shooting starAfter a declineTentative bullish
    MarubozuAll body, little or no wickBreakouts, trend legsConviction that may carry into the next session

    The same shape means opposite things in different places: a long lower wick after a fall is a hammer, after a rally a hanging man. Shape alone never sets direction.

    Two- and three-candle patterns#

    PatternCandlesMeaningWeight
    Harami2Small body inside a big one: momentum stallsWeak, watch
    Engulfing2Second body covers the first: control changesModerate
    Tweezer top / bottom2+Same high or low rejected twiceModerate at a level
    Morning / evening star3Takeover plus confirmationHigher
    Three soldiers / crows3Strong momentumConfirms, often late

    More candles demand more agreement, so they carry more weight, but reliability stays modest. One winning example (a bullish engulfing at a swing low followed by +5.7% in 10 sessions) is an illustration, not a base rate.

    The three context filters#

    FilterStrongWeak
    LevelSupport, resistance, prior high or low, round number, Fibonacci levelOpen space
    TrendWith the larger trend, at the end of a pullbackAgainst an extended trend
    VolumeAbove averageThin, sleepy session

    A hammer in the middle of nowhere means nothing; the same hammer at support, in an uptrend, on heavy volume is a setup. Counter-trend reversal patterns fail far more often.

    Pattern discipline#

    1. Mark support, resistance and trend on a clean chart before looking for patterns.
    2. Use a fixed, written definition (for example, hammer lower wick at least 2× the body).
    3. Count independent reasons: one is weak, three is a setup.
    4. Define stop and target before entering.
    5. Log every pattern and outcome; most patterns barely beat the +1.48% per 20 days baseline, and a journal exposes your bias.

    04Chart patterns: reversals, continuations and gaps

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 13–15 (reversal patterns, continuation patterns, gaps)

    Reversal patterns#

    PatternShapeConfirms when
    Head and shoulders topMiddle peak highest; right shoulder below the headClose below the neckline
    Inverse head and shouldersMiddle trough lowestClose above the neckline
    Double top (M)Two similar highsClose below the middle dip
    Double bottom (W)Two similar lowsClose above the middle peak
    Triple top / bottomThree tests of one levelBreak of the level; rarer, slower

    Prerequisites: a clear prior trend to reverse, a defined neckline, and follow-through after the break. Volume is typically heaviest on the left shoulder and head, lighter on the right, and expands on the break.

    Confirming a reversal, with a worked target#

    1. Confirm there is a prior trend.
    2. Draw the neckline before the last leg completes.
    3. Wait for a decisive close beyond it, not an intraday poke.
    4. Look for volume expansion; consider entering on the retest.
    5. Stop just back inside the pattern.
    measured target = neckline ± pattern heightdouble bottom: lows ≈ 1,080, neckline ≈ 1,270height ≈ 190 → target ≈ 1,270 + 190 = 1,460

    The target is a planning figure for reward against risk, not a promise.

    Continuation patterns#

    PatternShapeTypical leanMeasured move
    Bull / bear flagSmall drifting rectangle after a sharp moveWith the flagpoleFlagpole height from the breakout
    PennantSmall triangle contracting to a pointWith the trendFlagpole height
    Ascending triangleFlat top, rising lowsOften upWidest height
    Descending triangleFlat bottom, falling highsOften down; can break up in an uptrendWidest height
    Symmetrical triangleLower highs, higher lowsNeutral; the trend guides itWidest height
    RectangleFlat range inside a trendAmbiguous while formingBox height

    Flags and pennants last days to a few weeks. A healthy pause is shallow and orderly, with volume drying up inside and expanding on the break; a deep, accelerating pullback or volume swelling against the trend says reversal instead. If the target is barely larger than the stop distance, skip the trade.

    Gaps: four types#

    TypeWhereTypical behaviour
    CommonInside a rangeSmall, fills quickly
    BreakawayOut of a base or patternStarts a trend, often doesn't fill
    RunawayMid-trendAcceleration, near the halfway point
    ExhaustionEnd of an extended moveFinal leap, usually fills, then reverses

    Breakaway and exhaustion gaps look identical on the day: the trend's age and the next few sessions tell them apart. An island reversal (bars stranded between two gaps) signals a likely flip.

    Gap statistics and rules#

    • Nifty 50 daily: gaps over 2% filled within 20 sessions about 78–79% of the time; gaps over 5% about 71–73%. Bigger gaps fill less often, so fading them is a bet against the odds.
    • Heavy volume suggests conviction (breakaway or runaway); light volume suggests a common gap.
    • Read every gap with four questions: what news, how big (%), where in the trend, and how much volume.
    • Don't chase the open; let the first range form. Treat the previous close and the gap open as levels.
    • Size down on gap days: wider stops need smaller positions to keep rupee risk constant.
    gap % = (open − previous close) ÷ previous close × 1001,200 → 1,290: 90 points = 7.5%

    05Indicators: averages, momentum, volatility, volume and trend strength

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 16–24 (moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger and ATR, volume, trend strength, trend tools, combining, toolbox)

    Moving averages#

    SMA = sum of the last N closes ÷ Neach new bar: add the newest close, drop the oldest
    Length (daily)CoversUse
    20About a monthShort-term swing direction
    50About a quarterIntermediate trend
    200About a yearLong-term health
    • SMA weighs every bar equally: calmer, more lag. EMA weighs recent bars more: turns sooner, more false twitches. The lag trade-off can be chosen, never removed.
    • Read the slope first (rising, flat, falling), then price versus the line. Slope beats the exact touch.
    • A 50-day on a daily chart is not a 50-day on a weekly chart: always state the timeframe.

    Golden and death crosses, tested#

    Signal (Nifty 50, 16 years)Up after 60 daysTypical 60-day move
    Golden cross (527 cases)59%+2.7%
    Death cross (529 cases)66%+4.8%
    Baseline drift—~+4.5%
    • On this data "golden = buy, death = sell" is close to backwards; golden-cross winners often dipped about 7% first.
    • A dip to a rising 50-day average gave +0.67% over 10 days against a +0.58% baseline: an edge under 0.1 point.
    • Better use: a direction filter (longs only while the 50-day is above a rising 200-day), which mainly cuts drawdowns and time at risk.

    RSI: momentum, not a buy/sell switch#

    • RSI (default 14) compares recent up-move strength with down-move strength on a 0–100 scale; 50 is balance, 70/30 the usual reference lines. It compares a stock only with its own history.
    • Shorter lookbacks (7) are faster and noisier; longer (21) are smoother.
    • In a range RSI swings between extremes; in an uptrend it tends to hold about 60–80 (a break below 40 is a warning); in a downtrend about 20–40.
    RSI bucket (Nifty 50)Average next 20 days
    Any day (baseline)+1.48%
    Below 30+1.8%
    50–70 and 70–80+1.4%
    Above 80+2.2% (positive 60% of the time)

    Overbought streaks lasted up to 43 days and oversold up to 44: extremes are states, often of persistent strength, not reversal signals.

    Divergence and the oscillator family#

    • Bearish divergence: price makes a higher high, RSI a lower high. Bullish: price a lower low, RSI a higher low. It can last weeks; use it at extremes near levels, and let a price trigger (broken trendline, reversal candle) time the entry.
    • Stochastic: where the close sits in the recent range; above 80 overbought, below 20 oversold.
    • Williams %R: an inverted stochastic, 0 to −100.
    • CCI: distance from the recent average, unbounded; ±100 marks "stretched".
    • Stochastic RSI: a stochastic of RSI; the most sensitive and noisiest.

    Five oscillators on the same prices are one opinion repeated, not five confirmations.

    MACD#

    MACD line = EMA12 − EMA26signal line = 9-period EMA of the MACD linehistogram = MACD line − signal line
    ReadingMeaning
    MACD crosses above / below signalMomentum turning up / down
    MACD above / below zeroTrend leans up / down
    Histogram growingMomentum accelerating
    Histogram shrinkingMomentum fading: early warning before a cross
    DivergenceTrend tiring: tighten risk, wait for price

    MACD is in rupees, so compare shapes and crossings, not raw values across stocks.

    MACD crossovers, tested#

    • 7,640 bullish crosses on Nifty 50 stocks over 16 years: 58% positive after 30 days; 37% gained more than 5%.
    • Filtering for "MACD above zero" did not meaningfully raise the win rate.
    • Strong in trends (the histogram flags fatigue early), weak in sideways markets (repeated whipsaw crosses), and always lagging.
    • Practical use: confirm the trend with price and averages first, use a bullish cross near support to time a continuation entry, and watch the histogram for fading momentum.

    Volume: relative volume and spikes#

    relative volume = today's volume ÷ 20-day average volume~1 = ordinary · 2 = double · 5–6+ = major event
    • Spike plus a decisive close: conviction in the close's direction.
    • Spike at the end of a long trend: possible climax.
    • Spike with a flat close: churn, a draw; not a signal.
    • Healthy uptrend: volume swells on up-days and fades on pullbacks. Rallies on shrinking volume or selloffs on rising volume are warnings.
    • Volume confirms moves; it doesn't start them.

    OBV, MFI, A/D and CMF#

    ToolBuildsRead
    OBVAdds volume on up days, subtracts on down daysIgnore the level; check slope and agreement with price
    MFIVolume-weighted RSI, 0–100Above 80 / below 20 stretched; divergence is its best signal
    A/D lineWeights volume by where the close sits in the day's rangeClose near the high adds most
    CMFAbout 20-day accumulation/distribution oscillator around zeroAbove zero: buying pressure dominates

    They all measure the same idea, so pick one you trust. In India, add delivery %: two stocks both up 3% on 4× volume mean different things at 70% delivery (holders accumulating) and 15% (intraday churn).

    Bollinger Bands and ATR#

    middle band = 20-day SMA; bands = middle ± 2 standard deviationsbandwidth = (upper − lower) ÷ middle (small = squeeze)true range = max(high − low, |high − prev close|, |low − prev close|)ATR = 14-day average of true range
    • Volatility tools measure how much price moves, never which way.
    • A squeeze (very low bandwidth, e.g. about 4.7% before one stock's expansion) says a bigger move is due, not its direction.
    • Walking the band: strong trends hug an outer band. A close above the upper band still averaged +0.58% over 10 days, so it is strength, not a sell signal.
    • ATR sets stops: 2–3× ATR from entry. A stock with ATR ≈ ₹85 (2.8% of price) needs about ₹170 of room at 2×.

    Use one volatility tool, not the whole set.

    Trend strength: ADX, DMI and Aroon#

    • ADX (0–100) measures strength only, not direction: below 20 weak or choppy, above 25 a real trend, rising means gaining force, around 40+ very strong and possibly mature.
    • DMI: +DI (upward pressure) above −DI means buyers lead. A +DI cross with ADX above 25 means much more than the same cross with ADX below 20.
    • Aroon Up / Down (0–100): how recently a new high or low was made. Up near 100 with Down low is a fresh uptrend; both mid-range is sideways.
    • ADX lags, so it is a context filter, never a timing trigger; a very high ADX can mark a stretched trend near its end.

    "Direction tells you which way; strength tells you whether to bother."

    Trend or range? Pick the tactic#

    OverlayHow it worksBest inFails in

    Check ADX first: above about 25, read the overlay's side and trail the stop with it; below about 20, ignore the flips. Stacking all three is one opinion drawn three ways.

    Stacking indicators without fooling yourself#

    FamilyQuestionJob in the planPick one
    TrendWhich way?Filter: no buys unless above a rising long average50-day MA, Supertrend
    MomentumHow strong and fast?Timing: momentum turning up after a dipRSI, MACD
    VolumeWho is behind it?Conviction: above-average volumeVolume vs 20-day average, OBV
    VolatilityHow much?Risk: stop distance and sizeATR, bandwidth
    • Name a tool's family before adding it; if you already own that family, skip it. Fill missing families first (usually volume and volatility). Four tools maximum; most charts need one or two.
    • If two indicators always fire together, one is clutter. Conflict (trend up, momentum overbought) is information: wait for a pullback.
    • Price beats any oscillator: a break of a major level on heavy volume overrules "overbought".

    A clean chart template#

    1. Candlesticks: the primary signal.
    2. Hand-drawn support, resistance and trendlines.
    3. One trend tool, for example the 50-day average.
    4. One volume read: bars with a 20-day average, or OBV.
    5. Optional: one momentum or one volatility tool, not both.

    You should be able to state the job of every line on your chart.

    06From analysis to action: timeframes, systems, risk and discipline

    OpenAlgo Open Varsity · Technical Analysis · chapters 25–28 (multiple timeframes, building a strategy, risk management, psychology and next steps)

    Top-down: two timeframes, two jobs#

    • Never decide from one timeframe. Use two about 4–5× apart (weekly and daily for swing trading): one weekly candle ≈ five daily.
    • Weekly = direction: structure (higher highs and lows?), price versus a long average and its slope, major support and resistance.
    • Daily = timing: location (a pullback to support or a rising average, not stretched) and a trigger in the direction the weekly allows.
    • If they conflict, the larger timeframe wins, which usually means no trade. A 4% two-day drop looks severe on the daily and minor on the weekly.

    From setup to system#

    QuestionFixed before trading
    ContextWhen is a trade allowed? (trend, timeframe, market mood)
    TriggerWhat exact event enters?
    ExitTarget or trailing rule when right; stop when wrong
    SizeHow much to commit, from the stop
    1. Write the rule so a stranger would take the same trades.
    2. Backtest across many stocks and years, including costs.
    3. Read the equity curve and worst drawdown, not just the headline return.
    4. Resist tuning; prefer few rules and round numbers (50/200, not 47/213), and check on data the rule never saw.
    5. Decide what you are buying: return, smoothness, or time out of the market.

    Expectancy beats win rate#

    expectancy = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss0.40 × ₹2 − 0.60 × ₹1 = +₹0.20 per ₹1 risked
    • A 40% win rate is profitable when winners are about twice the losers: cut losers at 1R, let winners run to 2–3R.
    • The course's 50/200 trend filter on 48 stocks: +107% median return versus +744% for buy-and-hold, drawdown −47% versus −56%, invested about 55% of the time. Protection from big falls also costs you the sharpest recoveries.
    • Be suspicious of a silky-smooth, loss-free backtest; it is usually curve-fitted.

    Position size from the stop#

    1. Put the stop where the trade's reason is invalidated, a little beyond the level, never exactly on it.
    2. Set the risk budget: 1–2% of capital.
    3. Check the target is at least about 2× the stop distance; below 1:1, pass.
    4. Size = budget ÷ (entry − stop). Execute and honour the stop; never widen it.
    entry 322.8, stop 300 → R = 22.8; target 380 → R:R ≈ 2.5₹10,00,000 × 1% = ₹10,000 → 10,000 ÷ 22.8 ≈ 438 sharesposition ≈ ₹1.41 lakh (14% of capital), loss if stopped = ₹10,000

    Survival maths#

    DrawdownGain needed to recover
    −10%+11%
    −20%+25%
    −33%+50%
    −50%+100%
    −75%+300%
    −90%+900%

    At 1% risk, ten straight losses cost about 10%. At 20% risk, three losses cost about half the account. Risk is the input; position size is the output.

    Emotions and the rules they break#

    EmotionRule it breaksResult
    FearLet winners run; honour stopsSmall wins, big losses
    GreedFixed 1% riskA 5% bet means one loss costs five planned losses
    FOMOWait for your entryBuying after a 6% news gap, where odds are weakest
    OvertradingTrade only your setupsCosts erase a thin edge
    Curve-fittingKeep rules simpleFalse confidence

    Most retail losses come from behaviour, not analysis: thin edges minus costs, emotional errors and overtrading usually land below zero. Discipline is learnable; prediction is not.

    Plan, execute, journal#

    flowchart TD
  P["Plan, written while calm"] --> E["Execute only planned setups"]
  E --> J["Journal every trade"]
  J --> R["Review the last 20 trades on process"]
  R --> P
    • Plan: instruments, timeframe, setups, exact entry, stop, target and minimum reward-to-risk, 1–2% risk, and conduct rules (daily loss cap, no chasing gaps, no widening stops).
    • Journal: setup, reason, stop and target, plan followed (yes or no), emotion.
    • Review: grade process, not profit. A winner taken on a whim is still a bad trade.

    07Checklists and one-line formulas

    Run these before every trade and at every weekly review.

    Before the trade#

    • Weekly direction allows it; daily location and trigger agree.
    • The setup sits at a level marked in advance, with the trend, on above-average volume.
    • ADX says trend tools or range tactics, and I'm using the right ones.
    • Stop beyond the invalidation level; target at least about 2× the stop.
    • Size = 1–2% of capital ÷ (entry − stop).
    • No major news or results before the planned exit.

    Honest-signal checklist#

    • Compared with the +1.48% per 20 days baseline, does this signal add anything?
    • Is it one tool per family, or the same opinion repeated?
    • Has the rule been tested on many stocks and years, with costs?
    • Would I take the same trade if I wrote the rule down first?
    • Am I chasing a breakout, or waiting for the retest?
